Exercise 1 — The hydrogen atom according to Bohr sheet

  1. Write, for the two forces, the condition that keeps the electron on its trajectory.
  2. Deduce the radius r of the orbit travelled by the electron.
  3. Write the expression of the possible values of the system's total energy.
  4. Why is the value n = 0 of the quantum number forbidden?
  5. Calculate the energies (in eV) of the first three energy states.
  6. Of these three states, from which is it easiest to remove the electron from the hydrogen atom?
  7. What is the ionization energy of the hydrogen atom in its ground state?
  • h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s
  • me = 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg
  • e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
  • k = 9 × 10⁹ SI
Hint

Two forces act on the electron: the centrifugal force and the electrostatic attraction of the nucleus. Set them equal, then combine with Bohr's quantization of the angular momentum to eliminate v. Energy = kinetic + potential; once you have rn, En follows.

Solution

1) Equilibrium condition

  1. Law Stable orbit p. 13
    Fc = mv²r (centrifugal) ; Fa = k(−e)(+e)r² (attraction)

    For a stationary orbit the two forces cancel: the vectors add up to zero, Fa + Fc = 0, so their magnitudes are equal.

    ‖Fc‖ = ‖Fa‖ ⇒ mv²/r = ke²/r² ⇒ mv² = ke²/r (1)
mv²/r = ke²/r², i.e. mv² = ke²/r

2) Radius of the orbit

  1. Law Bohr's 2nd postulate p. 11
    mvr = n h2π
    m²v²r² = n²h²/4π² ⇒ mv² = n²h² / (4π²mr²) (2)
  2. Law Orbit radius p. 13–14
    (1) = (2) ⇒ ke²/r = n²h² / (4π²mr²)
    rn = n² · h² / (4kπ²me²) = a0 n²
    a0 = (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴)² / (4 × 9 × 10⁹ × π² × 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ × (1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹)²) = 5.29 × 10⁻¹¹ m = 0.529 Å
rn = h²4kπ²me² n² = 0.529 n² Å

3) Total energy

  1. Law Total energy p. 15–16
    ET = Ec + Ep ; Ec = mv²/2 ; Ep = −ke²/r
    with (1), Ec = ke²/2r ⇒ ET = ke²/2r − ke²/r = −ke²/2r
    replace r by rn: En = − (2k²π²me⁴ / h²) · 1/n²
    E1 = − 2 × (9 × 10⁹)² × π² × 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ × (1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹)⁴ / (6.626 × 10⁻³⁴)² = −2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸ J = −13.6 eV
En = − 2k²π²me⁴h² · 1n² = −13.6n² eV (n = 1, 2, 3 …)

4) Why n = 0 is forbidden

With n = 0: r0 = a0 × 0² = 0 — the electron would sit on the nucleus (and mvr = 0: no orbital motion), and E = −13.6/0² is not defined (→ −∞). The atom would not exist, so n is a whole number ≥ 1.

n = 0 gives r = 0 (electron in the nucleus) and an infinite energy: impossible, n ≥ 1

5) The first three energy states

  1. Law Energy levels p. 17–18
    En = −13.6 / n² (eV)
    E1 = −13.6 / 1 = −13.6 eV
    E2 = −13.6 / 4 = −3.40 eV
    E3 = −13.6 / 9 = −1.51 eV
E1 = −13.6 eV · E2 = −3.40 eV · E3 = −1.51 eV

6) Easiest state to ionize

Removing the electron means bringing it to E∞ = 0; the energy needed is 0 − En = 13.6/n² eV: 13.6, 3.40 and 1.51 eV. The smallest is for n = 3 — the farthest orbit, where the electron is least bound.

From n = 3 (only 1.51 eV needed)

7) Ionization energy from the ground state

Ei = E∞ − E1 = 0 − (−13.6) = 13.6 eV = 13.6 × 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸ J
Ei = 13.6 eV ≈ 2.18 × 10⁻¹⁸ J

With the sheet's constants the full formula gives −13.64 eV; the lesson rounds it to −13.6 eV, used everywhere below.

Traps
  • Forgetting the minus sign: a bound electron has a negative energy; E∞ = 0 is the ionized state.
  • Thinking the lowest level is the easiest to ionize: it's the most stable, so the hardest.
  • Squaring n: E2 = −13.6/4, not −13.6/2.

Exercise 2 — Electrons ejected from sodium sheet

To eject electrons from a sodium metal surface, a mercury-vapour lamp emitting a line at 2537 Å is used.

  1. Calculate the energy of the incident photon.
  2. What will be the energy of an ejected electron, given that the threshold energy needed to remove an electron from a sodium surface is 2.3 eV?
  3. What is the nature of this energy (potential, kinetic or other)?
  4. What is the wavelength of the wave that de Broglie's theory associates with this electron?
  • λ = 2537 Å = 2.537 × 10⁻⁷ m
  • W0 = 2.3 eV
  • h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s
  • c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s
  • me = 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg
Hint

Photon energy from its wavelength (convert Å to m). Part of that energy pulls the electron out of the metal (the threshold); the rest is carried by the moving electron. From that energy find its speed, then use de Broglie.

Solution

1) Energy of the photon

  1. Law Photon energy p. 6
    E = hc / λ
    E = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.0 × 10⁸ / 2.537 × 10⁻⁷ = 7.84 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
    E = 7.835 × 10⁻¹⁹ / 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 4.89 eV
E ≈ 7.84 × 10⁻¹⁹ J ≈ 4.89 eV

2) Energy of the ejected electron

The photon gives all its energy to one electron: 2.3 eV are used to tear it from the metal, the remainder stays with the electron.

Ec = E − W0 = 4.89 − 2.3 = 2.59 eV = 2.59 × 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 4.15 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
Ec ≈ 2.59 eV ≈ 4.15 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

3) Nature of this energy

The electron is free and moving away from the surface: its energy is kinetic, Ec = ½mv².

Kinetic energy

4) De Broglie wavelength

  1. Law Kinetic energy p. 15
    Ec = mv²/2 ⇒ v = √(2Ec/m)
    v = √(2 × 4.151 × 10⁻¹⁹ / 9.109 × 10⁻³¹) = 9.55 × 10⁵ m/s
  2. Law De Broglie p. 7
    λ = h / mv
    λ = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ / (9.109 × 10⁻³¹ × 9.546 × 10⁵) = 7.62 × 10⁻¹⁰ m = 7.62 Å
λ ≈ 7.62 × 10⁻¹⁰ m = 7.62 Å (v ≈ 9.55 × 10⁵ m/s)
The threshold relation Ec = hν − W0 (photoelectric effect, Einstein) is not in your lesson yet — it's the standard energy balance the sheet expects. The English version of the sheet drops the "2.3 eV"; it is taken from the French version.
Traps
  • 1 Å = 10⁻¹⁰ m (not 10⁻⁹): 2537 Å = 253.7 nm.
  • Mixing J and eV in E − W0: convert both to the same unit first.
  • Using the photon's energy (4.89 eV) for the electron's speed: only what's left after the threshold becomes kinetic.
  • Using the photon's λ = hc/E for the electron: de Broglie uses its momentum mv.

Exercise 3 — An X photon ejects an inner electron sheet

An X photon of wavelength 150 pm ejects an electron from an inner shell of an atom. The ejected electron has a speed of 2.1 × 10⁷ m/s. What is the energy of the photon, and the (kinetic) energy of the electron — and its energy in the atom?

  • λ = 150 pm = 1.50 × 10⁻¹⁰ m
  • v = 2.1 × 10⁷ m/s
  • me = 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg
Hint

Photon: E = hc/λ. Ejected electron: ½mv². The difference between the two is what it cost to pull the electron out of its shell — its binding energy in the atom.

Solution
  1. Law Photon energy p. 6
    E = hc / λ
    E = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.0 × 10⁸ / 1.50 × 10⁻¹⁰ = 1.33 × 10⁻¹⁵ J = 8.27 × 10³ eV
  2. Law Kinetic energy p. 15
    Ec = mv²/2
    Ec = ½ × 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ × (2.1 × 10⁷)² = 2.01 × 10⁻¹⁶ J = 1.25 × 10³ eV

  3. Energy balance: the photon's energy = energy to free the electron + kinetic energy it leaves with.

    Ebinding = E − Ec = 1.325 × 10⁻¹⁵ − 2.009 × 10⁻¹⁶ = 1.12 × 10⁻¹⁵ J = 7.02 × 10³ eV

    So the electron's energy in its shell was E = −1.12 × 10⁻¹⁵ J ≈ −7.02 keV (bound ⇒ negative).

Ephoton ≈ 1.33 × 10⁻¹⁵ J (8.27 keV) · Ec ≈ 2.01 × 10⁻¹⁶ J (1.25 keV) · energy in the atom ≈ −1.12 × 10⁻¹⁵ J (−7.02 keV)
The question is garbled on the sheet ("its kinetic energy of the electron in the atom"). The page gives the kinetic energy of the ejected electron and the electron's (binding) energy in the atom, Ec − Ephoton. The balance E = W + Ec is the same as in Ex. 2 — not in the lesson yet.
Traps
  • 1 pm = 10⁻¹² m: 150 pm = 1.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ m (= 1.5 Å).
  • Forgetting the ½ in ½mv².
  • Squaring the speed: (2.1 × 10⁷)² = 4.41 × 10¹⁴.

Exercise 4 — Absorption from the 1st to the 5th excited state sheet

  1. Calculate the absorption energy, in eV and in J, of a transition from the first excited state to the fifth excited state of the hydrogen atom.
  2. Represent this transition on an energy diagram.
  3. Calculate the wavelength, and the speed associated with this wavelength.
  • hydrogen: En = −13.6/n² eV
  • 1st excited state, 5th excited state
  • h, c, 1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
Hint

The ground state is n = 1, so the k-th excited state is n = k + 1. ΔE = Efinal − Einitial (positive for absorption), then λ = hc/ΔE.

Solution

1) Absorption energy

  1. Ground state n = 1 ⇒ 1st excited state n = 2, 5th excited state n = 6.

  2. Law Bohr's 3rd postulate p. 11 · levels p. 17
    ΔE = |E6 − E2| ; En = −13.6/n² eV
    E2 = −13.6/4 = −3.40 eV ; E6 = −13.6/36 = −0.378 eV
    ΔE = −0.378 − (−3.40) = 3.02 eV
    ΔE = 3.022 × 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 4.84 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
ΔE ≈ 3.02 eV ≈ 4.84 × 10⁻¹⁹ J (absorbed)

2) Energy diagram

3) Wavelength and "speed"

  1. Law Photon energy p. 6
    ΔE = hc / λ ⇒ λ = hc / ΔE
    λ = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.0 × 10⁸ / 4.842 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 4.11 × 10⁻⁷ m = 411 nm (violet, visible)
  2. Law Frequency p. 3
    ν = c / λ
    ν = 3.0 × 10⁸ / 4.106 × 10⁻⁷ = 7.31 × 10¹⁴ Hz

    The light itself travels at c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.

λ ≈ 411 nm · ν ≈ 7.31 × 10¹⁴ Hz (speed of the radiation = c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s)
"The speed associated with this wavelength" is ambiguous. Read as the frequency ν (the usual pair λ, ν) — given above. If your teacher means the speed of an electron whose de Broglie wavelength is 411 nm: v = h/(mλ) = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ / (9.109 × 10⁻³¹ × 4.106 × 10⁻⁷) ≈ 1.77 × 10³ m/s.
Traps
  • "5th excited state" is n = 6, not n = 5 (the ground state is n = 1, not an excited state).
  • ΔE in eV in λ = hc/ΔE: convert to joules first.
  • Sign: absorption ⇒ the electron goes up; ΔE > 0 is absorbed, not emitted.
