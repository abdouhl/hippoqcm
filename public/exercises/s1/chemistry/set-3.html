Exercise 1 — Energy levels of hydrogen sheet p. 1

The energy levels of the hydrogen atom are given by En = −13.6/n² (in eV).

  1. Calculate the values of the 4 lowest energy levels.
  2. Place the levels on a diagram.
  3. Which is the ground level?
  4. Consider the transition from level 3 to level 2.
    1. Represent this transition on the diagram. Is the radiation emitted or absorbed?
    2. Calculate the wavelength of this transition.
    3. To which region of light does this radiation belong?
  5. The atom absorbs a photon of wavelength λ = 121.7 nm.
    1. Which transition causes this absorption?
    2. Represent this transition on the diagram.

Data: h = 6.62 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m·s⁻¹, 1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, 1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m.

  • En = −13.6/n² eV
  • transition 3 → 2
  • λ = 121.7 nm absorbed
Hint

Put n = 1, 2, 3, 4 into the formula. A transition to a lower level releases energy. For the wavelength, compute ΔE in joules, then λ = hc/ΔE. For 5, compute the photon's energy in eV and look for the two levels whose energy difference matches it.

Solution

1) The four lowest levels

  1. Law Energy levels p. 17
    En = − 13.6 / n² (eV)
    E1 = −13.6/1 = −13.6 eV
    E2 = −13.6/4 = −3.40 eV
    E3 = −13.6/9 = −1.51 eV
    E4 = −13.6/16 = −0.85 eV
E₁ = −13.6 eV · E₂ = −3.40 eV · E₃ = −1.51 eV · E₄ = −0.85 eV

2) Diagram (with the transitions of 4a and 5b)

3) Ground level

The ground level is the lowest one (the most stable): n = 1, E₁ = −13.6 eV.

Ground state: n = 1 (E₁ = −13.6 eV)

4) Transition 3 → 2

  1. a) The electron goes from n = 3 (−1.51 eV) down to n = 2 (−3.40 eV): the atom loses energy, so a photon is emitted (red arrow).

  2. Law Transition p. 11 · photon energy p. 6
    ΔE = |E3 − E2| = hcλ
    ΔE = |−1.51 − (−3.40)| = 13.6 × (1/4 − 1/9) = 1.89 eV
    ΔE = 1.89 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 3.02 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
    λ = 6.62 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸3.02 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 6.57 × 10⁻⁷ m = 657 nm
  3. Law Visible spectrum p. 4
    390 nm ≤ λ ≤ 760 nm

    c) 390 < 657 < 760 nm, so the radiation is visible (red).

a) emitted · b) λ = 657 nm · c) visible light (red)

5) Absorption of λ = 121.7 nm

  1. Law Photon energy p. 6
    E = hc / λ
    E = 6.62 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸121.7 × 10⁻⁹ = 1.63 × 10⁻¹⁸ J = 1.63 × 10⁻¹⁸1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 10.2 eV

  2. The atom starts in its ground state (n = 1). After absorbing the photon:

    Efinal = E1 + 10.2 = −13.6 + 10.2 = −3.40 eV = E2
    Check: E2 − E1 = −3.40 + 13.6 = 10.2 eV ✓
a) transition n = 1 → n = 2 (absorption) · b) green arrow on the diagram
Trap Putting ΔE in eV directly into λ = hc/ΔE (convert to J first, × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹). Writing λ in m and comparing it to the visible range in nm. Calling 3 → 2 an absorption: going down = emission.

Exercise 2 — Hydrogen: lines, Balmer series, unknown levels sheet p. 1

We study a hydrogen atom using the Bohr model.

  1. The hydrogen atom is excited to level n = 5:
    1. How many lines can be emitted when it returns to the ground state?
    2. What is the radius of the ground-state orbit?
  2. Calculate the wavelength and the energy of the first four lines and of the last line of the Balmer series.
  3. The atom, in its first excited state (n₁), absorbs a photon of wavelength λ₁ = 410 nm and reaches a level n₂. Then, going from n₂ to a level n₃, it emits radiation of energy 0.472 eV. Find n₂ and n₃.
  • excited level n = 5
  • Balmer: lower level n = 2
  • n₁ = 2 (1st excited state)
  • λ₁ = 410 nm
  • emitted: 0.472 eV
Hint

1a) Count every possible jump downward between levels 5, 4, 3, 2, 1. 2) Balmer lines end on n = 2: the first ones come from n = 3, 4, 5, 6, the last from n = ∞. 3) Add the photon's energy to E₂ to find En₂, then subtract 0.472 eV to find En₃; turn each energy back into n with n² = 13.6/|E|.

Solution

1a) Number of emitted lines from n = 5

Counting the lines is not in your lessons yet. Each pair of levels between 1 and n gives one line, so the count is n(n − 1)/2.

  1. The electron can come down directly or by steps. Each pair of levels (upper → lower) gives one line:

    from 5: 5→4, 5→3, 5→2, 5→1 (4) · from 4: 4→3, 4→2, 4→1 (3) · from 3: 3→2, 3→1 (2) · from 2: 2→1 (1)
    N = 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 5 × 42 = 10 lines
10 lines

1b) Radius of the ground-state orbit

  1. Law Orbit radius p. 14
    rn = a0 n²
    r1 = a0 × 1² = 0.53 Å = 0.53 × 10⁻¹⁰ m
r₁ = a₀ = 0.53 Å (the lesson gives 0.529 Å, p. 17)

2) Balmer series

The series names are not in your lessons yet. The Balmer series is the set of lines that end on n = 2.

  1. Law Transition + photon energy p. 11
    ΔE = 13.6 (12² − 1n²) eV ; λ = hcΔE
    1st line, 3→2: ΔE = 13.6 × (1/4 − 1/9) = 1.89 eV = 3.02 × 10⁻¹⁹ J → λ = 1.986 × 10⁻²⁵ / 3.02 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 657 nm
    2nd line, 4→2: ΔE = 13.6 × (1/4 − 1/16) = 2.55 eV = 4.08 × 10⁻¹⁹ J → λ = 487 nm
    3rd line, 5→2: ΔE = 13.6 × (1/4 − 1/25) = 2.86 eV = 4.57 × 10⁻¹⁹ J → λ = 435 nm
    4th line, 6→2: ΔE = 13.6 × (1/4 − 1/36) = 3.02 eV = 4.84 × 10⁻¹⁹ J → λ = 411 nm
    last line, ∞→2: ΔE = 13.6 × 1/4 = 3.40 eV = 5.44 × 10⁻¹⁹ J → λ = 365 nm

657, 487, 435, 411 nm (visible) and 365 nm for the last line (near UV)

3) Finding n₂ and n₃

  1. Law Photon energy p. 6
    E = hc / λ
    E = 6.62 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸410 × 10⁻⁹ = 4.84 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 3.03 eV
  2. Law Absorption: En₂ = En₁ + hν p. 11
    En = −13.6/n² ⇒ n² = 13.6 / |En|
    En₂ = −3.40 + 3.03 = −0.373 eV
    n₂² = 13.6 / 0.373 = 36.5 ≈ 36 ⇒ n₂ = 6 (E6 = −0.378 eV)

  3. Emission: the atom loses 0.472 eV.

    En₃ = E6 − 0.472 = −0.378 − 0.472 = −0.850 eV
    n₃² = 13.6 / 0.850 = 16.0 ⇒ n₃ = 4

  4. Check: 410 nm is exactly the 4th Balmer line (6 → 2) found in question 2 ✓

n₂ = 6 · n₃ = 4
Trap Answering 4 lines for 1a (only the direct jumps 5→4, 5→3, 5→2, 5→1). Taking n₁ = 1: the “1st excited state” is n = 2. In question 3, use the exact E₆ = −0.378 eV for the next step, not the rounded −0.373 eV.

Exercise 3 — The hydrogen-like ion of boron sheet p. 1

We are interested in the hydrogen-like ion of boron (5B).

  1. What is the hydrogen-like ion of boron?
  2. What is the wavelength of the 2nd line of the Paschen series? Calculate the energy emitted during this transition.
  3. What is the ionization energy of this ion in eV?
  4. The electron of this ion, in a level n, absorbs a photon of wavelength 19.5 nm and reaches level 2n. What is the radius of the orbit of level n?
  5. Its electron is on a level n₁ of energy −37.8 eV. It absorbs radiation and reaches a level n₂ whose radius is 6.77 Å. Find n₁ and n₂, and λn₁,n₂.
  • Z = 5
  • Paschen: lower level n = 3
  • λ = 19.5 nm (n → 2n)
  • En₁ = −37.8 eV
  • rn₂ = 6.77 Å
Hint

A hydrogen-like ion keeps only one electron, so the Bohr formulas apply with the nucleus charge Z: energies × Z², radii ÷ Z. The 2nd Paschen line is 5 → 3. In 4, write ΔE between n and 2n as a function of n and solve for n.

Solution

Hydrogen-like ions are not in your lessons yet. The formulas En = −13.6 Z²/n² and rn = a0n²/Z are the Bohr model with a nucleus of charge +Ze (standard first-year chemistry).

1) The ion

Boron has Z = 5, so 5 electrons. Its hydrogen-like ion keeps only one electron, so it has lost 4: B⁴⁺.

B⁴⁺ (Z = 5, one electron)

2) 2nd line of the Paschen series

  1. Paschen = lines that end on n = 3. 1st line: 4 → 3; 2nd line: 5 → 3.

    Law Hydrogen-like levels + transition p. 11
    ΔE = 13.6 Z² (13² − 15²)
    ΔE = 13.6 × 25 × (1/9 − 1/25) = 340 × 0.0711 = 24.2 eV
    ΔE = 24.2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 3.87 × 10⁻¹⁸ J
    λ = 6.62 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸3.87 × 10⁻¹⁸ = 5.13 × 10⁻⁸ m = 51.3 nm
λ = 51.3 nm · emitted energy ΔE = 24.2 eV = 3.87 × 10⁻¹⁸ J

3) Ionization energy

  1. Ionization takes the electron from the ground state (n = 1) to n = ∞ (E = 0).

    Ei = 0 − E1 = 13.6 × Z² = 13.6 × 25 = 340 eV
Ei = 340 eV (25 times that of hydrogen)

4) Level n, then the radius rn

  1. Ephoton = 6.62 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸19.5 × 10⁻⁹ = 1.02 × 10⁻¹⁷ J = 63.7 eV
  2. ΔE(n → 2n) = 13.6 Z² (1n² − 14n²) = 340 × 34n² = 255n²
    n² = 255 / 63.7 = 4.01 ⇒ n = 2 (the electron goes 2 → 4)
  3. Law Radius, with Z
    rn = a0 n² / Z
    r2 = 0.53 × 2² / 5 = 0.424 Å
n = 2 · r₂ = 0.424 Å

5) n₁, n₂ and λn₁,n₂

  1. En₁ = −340 / n₁² = −37.8 ⇒ n₁² = 340 / 37.8 = 8.99 ⇒ n₁ = 3
  2. rn₂ = 0.53 × n₂² / 5 = 6.77 ⇒ n₂² = 6.77 × 5 / 0.53 = 63.9 ⇒ n₂ = 8
  3. ΔE = 340 × (1/9 − 1/64) = 32.5 eV = 5.19 × 10⁻¹⁸ J
    λ3,8 = 1.986 × 10⁻²⁵5.19 × 10⁻¹⁸ = 3.82 × 10⁻⁸ m = 38.2 nm
n₁ = 3 · n₂ = 8 · λ = 38.2 nm
Trap Forgetting Z² in the energy (that gives hydrogen's values, 25 times too small) or forgetting the ÷ Z in the radius. Taking 4 → 3 as the 2nd Paschen line: that is the 1st one. Writing B⁵⁺: that would have no electron left.

Exercise 4 — Wavelengths, Balmer extremes, an ion in the Sun sheet p. 1

  1. Calculate the wavelengths of the transitions λ4→3 and λ5→2 of the hydrogen atom.
  2. Calculate the extreme wavelengths of the Balmer series of the hydrogen atom. Represent these two electronic transitions on a diagram graduated in energy.
  3. The study of sunlight's spectrum reveals a series whose arrival level is n = 4, and one of its extreme wavelengths (the shortest) is λ∞ = 364.55 nm. Find Z and the ionized element ZX⁺ present in the Sun to which this series can be attributed.
  • transitions 4 → 3 and 5 → 2 (H)
  • Balmer: arrival level n = 2
  • series ending on n = 4: λmin = 364.55 nm
Hint

The longest wavelength of a series comes from the smallest energy jump (the next level down), and the shortest from the largest jump (from ∞). In 3, the shortest line is ∞ → 4: write its energy with Z² and solve for Z.

Solution

1) λ4→3 and λ5→2

  1. Law Transition + photon energy p. 11 · p. 6
    ΔE = |En1 − En2| ; λ = hc / ΔE
    4→3: ΔE = 13.6 × (1/9 − 1/16) = 0.661 eV = 1.06 × 10⁻¹⁹ J → λ = 1.986 × 10⁻²⁵ / 1.06 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 1.88 × 10⁻⁶ m = 1878 nm (infrared)
    5→2: ΔE = 13.6 × (1/4 − 1/25) = 2.86 eV = 4.57 × 10⁻¹⁹ J → λ = 435 nm (visible, violet-blue)
λ4→3 ≈ 1878 nm (1.88 µm) · λ5→2 = 435 nm

2) Extreme wavelengths of the Balmer series

  1. Balmer lines end on n = 2 (not in your lessons yet, see Ex. 2). The longest λ is the smallest jump (3 → 2); the shortest λ is the largest jump (∞ → 2).

    λmax = λ3→2: ΔE = 1.89 eV → λ = 657 nm
    λmin = λ∞→2: ΔE = 3.40 eV = 5.44 × 10⁻¹⁹ J → λ = 365 nm
λmax = 657 nm (3 → 2) · λmin = 365 nm (∞ → 2)

3) Z of the ion in the Sun

  1. The series ends on n = 4; its shortest line is ∞ → 4. For a hydrogen-like ion (not in your lessons yet):

    E = hcλ = 6.62 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸364.55 × 10⁻⁹ = 5.45 × 10⁻¹⁹ J = 3.40 eV
    E = 13.6 × Z² × (14² − 0) ⇒ Z² = 16 × 3.4013.6 = 4.01 ⇒ Z = 2

  2. Z = 2 is helium. With one electron, it is the ion He⁺, which fits ZX⁺. Hydrogen's own ∞ → 4 line would be at 4 × 364.55 = 1458 nm, so this series can't come from hydrogen.

Z = 2 → He⁺ (helium ion)
Trap Swapping the extremes: the largest energy jump gives the shortest wavelength. In 3, forgetting Z² and concluding it is hydrogen.

Exercise 5 — Absorbed, not absorbed, or ionized? sheet p. 1

  1. A hydrogen atom is in its ground state. It is irradiated with a monochromatic beam of wavelength λa = 97.25 nm. The experiment is repeated with λb = 101.33 nm, then λc = 82.67 nm. What happens in each case?
  2. The absorption spectrum of a hydrogen-like ion shows two wavelengths, 6.4 nm and 7.6 nm. What are the corresponding energies? What is the atomic number of this hydrogen-like ion, and which species is it?
  • H in ground state (n = 1, E₁ = −13.6 eV)
  • λa = 97.25 nm · λb = 101.33 nm · λc = 82.67 nm
  • ion: 6.4 nm and 7.6 nm absorbed
Hint

A photon is absorbed only if its energy lands the electron exactly on a level (or beyond 13.6 eV, which ionizes the atom). For each λ: E = hc/λ in eV, then E₁ + E, and check whether it equals a level −13.6/n².

Solution

1) The three beams

  1. Law Photon energy p. 6 · transition p. 11
    E = hc/λ ; absorbed only if E1 + E = En = −13.6/n²
    Possible jumps from n = 1: E₂ − E₁ = 10.20 eV · E₃ − E₁ = 12.09 eV · E₄ − E₁ = 12.75 eV · E₅ − E₁ = 13.06 eV · E∞ − E₁ = 13.6 eV

  2. λa = 97.25 nm

    E = 1.986 × 10⁻²⁵ / 97.25 × 10⁻⁹ = 2.04 × 10⁻¹⁸ J = 12.76 eV
    E₁ + E = −13.6 + 12.76 = −0.837 eV ⇒ n² = 13.6/0.837 = 16.3 ≈ 16 ⇒ n = 4 (E₄ = −0.85 eV)

    The energy matches 1 → 4 (12.75 eV, the small gap is rounding): the photon is absorbed and the atom is excited to n = 4.

  3. λb = 101.33 nm

    E = 1.986 × 10⁻²⁵ / 101.33 × 10⁻⁹ = 1.96 × 10⁻¹⁸ J = 12.25 eV
    E₁ + E = −13.6 + 12.25 = −1.35 eV ⇒ n² = 13.6/1.35 = 10.1 → n = 3.17, not an integer

    −1.35 eV falls between E₃ (−1.51) and E₄ (−0.85): no level. The photon is not absorbed and the atom stays in its ground state.

  4. λc = 82.67 nm

    E = 1.986 × 10⁻²⁵ / 82.67 × 10⁻⁹ = 2.40 × 10⁻¹⁸ J = 15.01 eV > 13.6 eV
    Ec (electron) = 15.01 − 13.6 = 1.41 eV

    The photon carries more than the ionization energy: the atom is ionized (H → H⁺ + e⁻). The extra 1.41 eV becomes the ejected electron's kinetic energy.

λa: absorbed, 1 → 4 · λb: not absorbed · λc: ionization, electron leaves with Ec ≈ 1.41 eV

2) The hydrogen-like ion

  1. E(6.4 nm) = 1.986 × 10⁻²⁵ / 6.4 × 10⁻⁹ = 3.10 × 10⁻¹⁷ J = 194 eV
    E(7.6 nm) = 1.986 × 10⁻²⁵ / 7.6 × 10⁻⁹ = 2.61 × 10⁻¹⁷ J = 163 eV

  2. An absorption spectrum starts from the ground state (n = 1). The two lines are the first two absorptions: the smaller energy (7.6 nm) is 1 → 2, the larger (6.4 nm) is 1 → 3. For a hydrogen-like ion (not in your lessons yet), En = −13.6 Z²/n²:

    1 → 2: 163 = 13.6 Z² (1 − 1/4) = 10.2 Z² ⇒ Z² = 16.0
    1 → 3: 194 = 13.6 Z² (1 − 1/9) = 12.09 Z² ⇒ Z² = 16.0 ✓
    Z = 4 → beryllium, with one electron: Be³⁺

The sheet doesn't say which transitions the two lines are. I assumed the first two absorptions from the ground state (1 → 2 and 1 → 3), since an absorption spectrum starts from n = 1. The energies would also fit 2 → 4 and 2 → 6 with Z = 8 (O⁷⁺), but that would need the ion to start in an excited state.

E = 194 eV (6.4 nm) and 163 eV (7.6 nm) · Z = 4 → Be³⁺
Trap Thinking that any photon with less than 13.6 eV is absorbed: it has to match a jump exactly. And a photon with more than 13.6 eV is not “too strong”: it ionizes the atom, and the surplus becomes kinetic energy.
