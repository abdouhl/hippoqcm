Exercise 1 — Drawing capsules sheet p. 1

I. A box contains nine capsules of a medication, all of the same size but of different colours, so that the draws are equiprobable. Four capsules are white, five are black. Three capsules are drawn simultaneously from the box. Compute the probability of each event:

  1. A: “All capsules are white”
  2. B: “The capsules are of different colours”
  3. C: “There are more white capsules than black capsules”

II. The same experiment is repeated, but this time the three capsules are drawn successively without replacement.

  1. Compute the probability that the three drawn capsules are of the same colour.
  2. Answer the same question if the three capsules are drawn with replacement after each draw.
  • 9 capsules: 4 white (W), 5 black (B)
  • 3 capsules drawn
  • I: simultaneous · II.1: one by one, without replacement · II.2: with replacement
Hint

Simultaneous draw → order doesn't count → count with combinations C93. For B, think of the opposite event. For C, “more white than black” among 3 means 3 white or 2 white + 1 black. In part II, multiply the probabilities draw by draw. The box changes after each draw only when there is no replacement.

Solution

I. Simultaneous draw

  1. Law Combinations · equiprobability
    P(E) = favourableC93
    Possible cases: C93 = 9 × 8 × 73 × 2 × 1 = 84

1) A: all three white

  1. Favourable: C43 = 4
    P(A) = 484 = 121 ≈ 0.048
P(A) = 1/21 ≈ 0.048

2) B: capsules of different colours

There are only two colours, so three capsules can never all be of three different colours. The event is read as “the capsules are not all of the same colour” (the draw contains both colours).

  1. Law Complement
    P(B) = 1 − P(B)

    B = “all the same colour” = 3 white or 3 black.

    C43 + C53 = 4 + 10 = 14
    P(B) = 1484 = 16
    P(B) = 1 − 16 = 56 ≈ 0.833

  2. Direct check: 2W + 1B or 1W + 2B → C42·C51 + C41·C52 = 6 × 5 + 4 × 10 = 70, and 70/84 = 5/6 ✓

P(B) = 5/6 ≈ 0.833

3) C: more white than black

  1. Among 3 capsules, “more white than black” means 3W (0B) or 2W + 1B.

    3W: C43 = 4
    2W + 1B: C42 × C51 = 6 × 5 = 30
    P(C) = 4 + 3084 = 3484 = 1742 ≈ 0.405
P(C) = 17/42 ≈ 0.405

II.1) Successively, without replacement: same colour

  1. Law Multiplication rule (conditional probabilities)
    P(W1 ∩ W2 ∩ W3) = P(W1) · P(W2 | W1) · P(W3 | W1 ∩ W2)

    After each draw there is one capsule fewer in the box, and one fewer of the colour drawn.

    P(WWW) = 49 × 38 × 27 = 24504 = 121
    P(BBB) = 59 × 48 × 37 = 60504 = 542
    P(same colour) = 24 + 60504 = 84504 = 16 ≈ 0.167

  2. This is the same result as P(B) in I.2. When you draw without replacement, drawing one by one or all at once gives the same probabilities for events that don't depend on the order.

P(same colour, without replacement) = 1/6 ≈ 0.167

II.2) With replacement: same colour

  1. The capsule is put back each time, so the box is the same at every draw (9 capsules, 4 W, 5 B) and the three draws are independent.

    P(WWW) = (4/9)³ = 64729
    P(BBB) = (5/9)³ = 125729
    P(same colour) = 64 + 125729 = 189729 = 727 ≈ 0.259
P(same colour, with replacement) = 7/27 ≈ 0.259
Trap Using arrangements (order counted) for a simultaneous draw: there the order doesn't count, so use Cnk. In II.1, forgetting to remove the drawn capsule (writing 4/9 × 4/9 × 4/9), which is the with-replacement calculation. For C, forgetting the case “3 white, 0 black”.

Exercise 2 — Influenza vaccine sheet p. 1

During an influenza epidemic, one third of the population is vaccinated. One out of 10 sick individuals is vaccinated, and the probability that a randomly chosen person is sick is 0.25. What is the probability that a vaccinated individual is sick nonetheless?

  • V = “vaccinated”, M = “sick”
  • P(V) = 1/3
  • P(V | M) = 1/10
  • P(M) = 0.25
  • Asked: P(M | V)
Hint

“One out of 10 sick individuals is vaccinated” is a probability given sick. You need the reverse conditional, so find P(V ∩ M) first, then divide by P(V).

Solution
  1. Law Multiplication rule
    P(V ∩ M) = P(V | M) · P(M)
    P(V ∩ M) = 0.1 × 0.25 = 0.025
  2. Law Conditional probability (Bayes)
    P(M | V) = P(V ∩ M)P(V)
    P(M | V) = 0.0251/3 = 0.025 × 3 = 0.075

  3. Check: 7.5 % of vaccinated people fall sick, against 25 % in the whole population. The vaccine reduces the risk, which makes sense.

P(M | V) = 0.075 = 7.5 %
Trap Confusing P(V | M) = 0.1 (the proportion of the sick who are vaccinated) with P(M | V), which is the question. Also, dividing by 3 instead of by 1/3.

Exercise 3 — Screening test for “Phona disease” sheet p. 1

I take a test to find out whether I am unknowingly affected by Phona disease.

  • If I am affected, the probability that the test is positive is 98 %;
  • If I am not affected, the probability that the test is positive is 6 %.

At my age, one person out of 150 is unknowingly affected; the remaining 149 are healthy. Let A be the event “I am affected” and T the event “the test is positive”.

  1. Express the information of the statement in terms of the events A, Ā, T and T.
  2. What is the probability that the test is positive and that I am not affected?
  3. Compute P(T).
  4. What is the probability that I am affected given that my test is positive?
  • P(T | A) = 0.98
  • P(T | Ā) = 0.06
  • P(A) = 1/150
Hint

Draw a probability tree: first branch A / Ā, second branch T / T. Question 2 is an intersection (multiply along one path), question 3 is the total probability (add the paths that end in T), and question 4 is Bayes.

Solution

1) The data as probabilities

  1. Law Complement (also within a condition)
    P(Ā) = 1 − P(A) ; P(T | A) = 1 − P(T | A)
    P(A) = 1150 ; P(Ā) = 149150
    P(T | A) = 0.98 ; P(T | A) = 0.02
    P(T | Ā) = 0.06 ; P(T | Ā) = 0.94
P(A) = 1/150, P(Ā) = 149/150, P(T | A) = 0.98, P(T | Ā) = 0.06 (and P(T | A) = 0.02, P(T | Ā) = 0.94)

2) Positive and not affected: P(T ∩ Ā)

  1. Law Multiplication rule
    P(T ∩ Ā) = P(T | Ā) · P(Ā)
    P(T ∩ Ā) = 0.06 × 149150 = 1492500 = 0.0596
P(T ∩ Ā) = 0.0596 ≈ 6 %

3) P(T)

  1. Law Total probability (partition A, Ā)
    P(T) = P(T | A) · P(A) + P(T | Ā) · P(Ā)
    P(T ∩ A) = 0.98 × 1150 = 0.006533
    P(T) = 0.006533 + 0.0596 = 0.06613
P(T) = 124/1875 ≈ 0.066

4) Affected given a positive test: P(A | T)

  1. Law Bayes' formula
    P(A | T) = P(T | A) · P(A)P(T)
    P(A | T) = 0.0065330.06613 = 49496 ≈ 0.0988

  2. Only ≈ 10 % of positive tests are real cases. The disease is rare (1/150), so the 6 % false positives among the 149 healthy people outnumber the true positives.

P(A | T) = 49/496 ≈ 0.099 (≈ 10 %)
Trap Answering 0.98 for question 4: that is P(T | A), not P(A | T). In question 2, answering 0.06: that is P(T | Ā), while the question asks for the intersection “positive and not affected”.

Exercise 4 — Defective products from three machines sheet p. 1–2

In a factory, machines A, B and C provide 25 %, 35 % and 40 % of the total production respectively. The percentages of defective products made by A, B and C are 5 %, 4 % and 2 % respectively. A product is selected at random from the total production and is found to be defective. What is the probability that it comes from machine A?

  • P(A) = 0.25, P(B) = 0.35, P(C) = 0.40
  • D = “defective”: P(D | A) = 0.05, P(D | B) = 0.04, P(D | C) = 0.02
  • Asked: P(A | D)
Hint

A, B and C form a partition (every product comes from exactly one machine). Find P(D) with the total probability formula, then reverse the condition with Bayes.

Solution
  1. Law Total probability
    P(D) = P(D | A)·P(A) + P(D | B)·P(B) + P(D | C)·P(C)
    P(D ∩ A) = 0.05 × 0.25 = 0.0125
    P(D ∩ B) = 0.04 × 0.35 = 0.014
    P(D ∩ C) = 0.02 × 0.40 = 0.008
    P(D) = 0.0125 + 0.014 + 0.008 = 0.0345
  2. Law Bayes' formula
    P(A | D) = P(D | A) · P(A)P(D)
    P(A | D) = 0.01250.0345 = 2569 ≈ 0.362

  3. Check: P(B | D) ≈ 0.406 and P(C | D) ≈ 0.232, and 0.362 + 0.406 + 0.232 = 1 ✓. Machine A makes only 25 % of the products but 36 % of the defective ones, because it has the highest defect rate.

P(A | D) = 25/69 ≈ 0.362 (≈ 36 %)
Trap Answering 0.25 (the share of production, i.e. before knowing the product is defective) or 0.05 (P(D | A)). Also, adding the percentages 5 + 4 + 2 instead of weighting each one by its machine's share.
