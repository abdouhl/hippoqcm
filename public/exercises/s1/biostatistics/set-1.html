Exercise 1 — Dental cavities in 94 students sheet

During a school medical examination, the number of dental cavities was recorded for 94 students of a school:

xi12345
ni242816188
  1. Determine the population, the studied variable, and specify its nature.
  2. Represent the series graphically and plot the increasing and decreasing cumulative frequency curves.
  3. Calculate the mean and the standard deviation.
  4. Determine the mode, the median and the three quartiles.
  • n = 94
  • xi = 1 … 5 cavities
  • ni = 24, 28, 16, 18, 8
Hint

A number of cavities is counted, so it only takes whole values: think about which kind of quantitative variable that is, and which graph goes with it. Build the statistical table first (ni, fi, nc↗, nc↘, nixi, nixi²) as in the lesson's Example 1 — every answer is read from it. For the median and quartiles, find the ranks first, then the value at that rank with nc↗.

Solution

1) Population, variable, nature

  1. Law Vocabulary and nature of variables p. 14–18
    quantitative discrete = countable, whole values

    Population: the 94 students of the school who were examined (the individual = one student). Variable: the number of dental cavities per student. It is expressed by a number that can only be a whole count (1, 2, 3…).

2) Graphs

  1. Law Graphical representations p. 26
    discrete variable → bar diagram (diagramme en bâtons)
  2. Law Cumulative frequencies p. 27
    nc↗ = n1 + … + ni ; nc↘ = nk + … + ni

    For a discrete variable the cumulative curves are step functions: the increasing one counts the students with ≤ x cavities and jumps at each value; the decreasing one counts the students with ≥ x cavities.

3) Mean and standard deviation

  1. Law Arithmetic mean p. 35
    x̄ = 1n Σ nixi
    x̄ = (1×24 + 2×28 + 3×16 + 4×18 + 5×8) / 94 = 240 / 94 = 2.553
  2. Law Variance p. 45
    V(X) = 1n Σ nixi² − x̄²
    V(X) = 768 / 94 − 2.553² = 8.170 − 6.519 = 1.651
    σX = √1.651 = 1.285
x̄ ≈ 2.55 cavities · σ ≈ 1.29 cavities (V ≈ 1.65)

4) Mode, median, quartiles

  1. Law Mode p. 29
    Mo = the value with the largest frequency
    largest ni = 28 ⇒ Mo = 2
  2. Law Median, discrete p. 39
    n = 2p ⇒ Me = (xp + xp+1) / 2
    n = 94 = 2 × 47 ⇒ Me = (x47 + x48) / 2

    From nc↗: values 1 occupy ranks 1–24, values 2 ranks 25–52. So x47 = x48 = 2.

    Me = (2 + 2) / 2 = 2
  3. Law Quartiles, discrete p. 48
    ranks n/4, n/2, 3n/4 (decimal rank → next whole rank)
    Q1: n/4 = 94/4 = 23.5 → 24th value; ranks 1–24 are 1 ⇒ Q1 = x24 = 1
    Q2 = Me = 2
    Q3: 3n/4 = 282/4 = 70.5 → 71st value; ranks 69–86 are 4 ⇒ Q3 = x71 = 4
Mo = 2 · Me = Q2 = 2 · Q1 = 1 · Q3 = 4 (IQR = 3)
Traps
  • Mode = 28: the mode is the value (2 cavities), not its frequency.
  • Median = x3 (the middle column of the table): the median is the middle student (ranks 47–48), found with nc↗.
  • Variance: forgetting to subtract x̄², or using Σnixi²/n as σ without the square root.
  • Drawing a histogram for a discrete variable: it's a bar diagram.

Exercise 2 — Heights of 100 adults sheet

The distribution of 100 adults according to their height (in cm) is given in the following table:

Classes[165, 170[[170, 175[[175, 180[[180, 185[[185, 190[[190, 195[[195, 200[
Frequencies81535201561
  1. What is the studied characteristic? State its nature.
  2. Represent the series graphically and plot the increasing and decreasing cumulative frequency curves.
  3. Calculate the mean and the standard deviation.
  4. Determine the mode, the median and the three quartiles.
  • n = 100
  • 7 classes of width a = 5 cm, from 165 to 200 cm
  • ni = 8, 15, 35, 20, 15, 6, 1
Hint

Height can take any value in an interval. Replace each class by its centre for x̄ and σ. For the mode, use the modal class and d1, d2; for the median and quartiles, find the class that contains rank n/2 (n/4, 3n/4) with nc↗ and interpolate inside it — exactly the lesson's hemoglobin example.

Solution

1) Characteristic and nature

  1. Law Nature of variables p. 18
    continuous quantitative: height, age, speed, weight…

    The characteristic is the height (in cm) of the 100 adults (the population). It is measured and can take any value in an interval, so the data are grouped in classes.

2) Graphs

  1. Law Histogram p. 24
    continuous variable → histogram: rectangles whose area ∝ ni

    All classes have the same width (5 cm), so the heights of the rectangles can simply be ni.

  2. Law Cumulative frequencies p. 27
    nc↗ plotted at the upper bound of each class ; nc↘ at the lower bound

    Increasing polygon: (165, 0), (170, 8), (175, 23), (180, 58), (185, 78), (190, 93), (195, 99), (200, 100). Decreasing polygon: (165, 100), (170, 92), (175, 77), (180, 42), (185, 22), (190, 7), (195, 1), (200, 0).

3) Mean and standard deviation

  1. Law Arithmetic mean p. 35–36
    x̄ = 1n Σ nixi (xi = class centre)
    x̄ = 17 955 / 100 = 179.55 cm
  2. Law Variance p. 45
    V(X) = 1n Σ nixi² − x̄²
    V(X) = 3 228 325 / 100 − 179.55² = 32 283.25 − 32 238.20 = 45.05 cm²
    σX = √45.05 = 6.71 cm
x̄ = 179.55 cm · σ ≈ 6.71 cm (V ≈ 45.05 cm²)

4) Mode, median, quartiles

  1. Law Mode, continuous p. 41–42
    Mo = ai + a · d1d1 + d2

    Modal class (largest ni = 35): [175, 180[.

    d1 = 35 − 15 = 20 ; d2 = 35 − 20 = 15
    Mo = 175 + 5 × 20 / (20 + 15) = 175 + 2.857 = 177.86 cm
  2. Law Median, continuous p. 44
    Me = ai + a · n/2 − nc,i−1↗nc,i↗ − nc,i−1↗
    n/2 = 50 ; nc↗ goes 23 → 58 in [175, 180[ ⇒ Me ∈ [175, 180[
    Me = 175 + 5 × (50 − 23) / (58 − 23) = 175 + 5 × 27/35 = 178.86 cm
  3. Law Quartiles, continuous p. 49
    same interpolation with n/4 and 3n/4
    Q1: n/4 = 25 ∈ [175, 180[ (23 → 58) ⇒ Q1 = 175 + 5 × (25 − 23) / 35 = 175.29 cm
    Q2 = Me = 178.86 cm
    Q3: 3n/4 = 75 ∈ [180, 185[ (58 → 78) ⇒ Q3 = 180 + 5 × (75 − 58) / 20 = 184.25 cm
Mo ≈ 177.86 cm · Me = Q2 ≈ 178.86 cm · Q1 ≈ 175.29 cm · Q3 = 184.25 cm (IQR ≈ 8.96 cm)

Check: Mo < Me < x̄ (177.9 < 178.9 < 179.6) — the tail of tall adults pulls the mean to the right.

In the lesson's hemoglobin example (p. 49), Q3 is printed as 130.33, but its own numbers give 130 + 10 × (42 − 38)/(50 − 38) = 133.33. The formula is right; trust it, not that result.
Traps
  • Using the lower bounds (165, 170…) instead of the class centres in x̄ and σ.
  • Mode: d1 and d2 are differences of frequencies with the neighbouring classes, and a is the class width (5), not the class's centre.
  • Median: nc,i−1↗ is the cumulative count before the class (23), not the frequency of the previous class (15).
  • Plotting nc↗ at the class centres: it goes at the upper bound (all adults below 175 cm = 23).
  • Losing digits in x̄² (179.55² = 32 238.2025): round only at the end, or the variance is wrong.
