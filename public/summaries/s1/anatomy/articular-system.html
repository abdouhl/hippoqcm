If you remember only this
  • Joint = junction between two or more skeletal components (bone–bone, cartilage–cartilage, bone–cartilage); studied by arthrology.
  • 3 types by the joining tissue: synarthroses = fibrous, immovable (sutures, interosseous membranes, distal tibiofibular) · amphiarthroses = cartilaginous, slightly movable, midline (intervertebral, pubic symphysis) · diarthroses = synovial, freely movable.
  • A diarthrosis has 4 structures: articular surfaces (covered with hyaline cartilage, at the epiphyses), joint cavity, retaining structures (capsule + passive ligaments + active ligaments = tendons), synovial membrane.
  • Synovial membrane lines the inside of the capsule and stops at the margins of the cartilages; synovia: waste resorption, nutrition of the avascular cartilage, lubrication.
  • 6 shapes: spheroid (3 axes), ellipsoid & saddle (2), ginglymus (pulley) & cylindrical (1), plane (limited gliding).
  • Adapting structures (fibrocartilage, only if surfaces are non-congruent): meniscus (mobile crescent, knee), labrum (fixed ring, glenoid), disc (complete partition, sternoclavicular).
  • Flexion ↓ angle, extension ↑ angle; abduction away from, adduction toward the median plane; circumduction = add + abd + flex + ext.

1. Introduction p. 1

Joint (articulation) The junction between two or more skeletal components. The scientific study of joints is arthrology.
A joint connects…Example
Two bonesSacroiliac joint
Two cartilagesLarynx
A bone and a cartilageSternochondral joint

Two primary roles: guiding and enabling movement; providing structural stability and consolidation of the skeletal components.

2. Classification of joints p. 1–2 2023-24 · 2024-25

Three anatomical types, based on the nature of the tissue joining the skeletal components:

TypeAlso calledJoining tissueExamples
SynarthrosisImmovable / fibrousFibrous connective tissue, may undergo secondary ossification/calcification Distal tibiofibular joint, interosseous membranes, cranial and facial sutures, fusion of the 3 parts of the hip bone (ilium, ischium, pubis)
AmphiarthrosisSlightly movable / cartilaginousCartilaginous tissue (fibrocartilaginous block); typically on the midline Intervertebral joints, pubic symphysis
DiarthrosisFreely movable / synovialSynovial joint: significant mobility, wide range of motionGlenohumeral, radiocarpal, humeroulnar… (see §4)
Don't mix up The distal tibiofibular joint and the interosseous membranes are synarthroses (fibrous), not amphiarthroses. The pubic symphysis and intervertebral joints are the amphiarthroses.

3. Diarthroses: anatomical description p. 2 2024-25 · 2025-26

A diarthrodial joint has four principal structures:

StructureWhat to know
1. Articular surfacesAt the epiphyses of long bones, or the angles and surfaces of flat bones; defined geometric shapes; covered with smooth articular (hyaline) cartilage. If the surfaces are incongruent, adapting fibrocartilaginous structures interpose (§5).
2. Joint cavityThe fluid-filled space separating the opposing articular surfaces.
3. Retaining structures (means of union)
  • Capsule: fibrous envelope enclosing the joint cavity.
  • Passive ligaments: fibrous bands that reinforce the capsule.
  • Active ligaments: tendons of the muscles adjacent to the joint; they stabilize and actively mobilize it.
4. Synovial membraneThe specialized gliding membrane; lines the internal surface of the capsule and attaches at the margins of the articular cartilages (does not cover them). Secretes synovial fluid (synovia), a viscous fluid.
Synovia — triple function
  1. Resorption of metabolic waste products.
  2. Nutrition of the avascular articular cartilage.
  3. Lubrication to reduce friction during movement.
Memory trick Synovia "R-N-L": it Removes, Nourishes, Lubricates.
Passive vs active Ligaments = passive (reinforce the capsule); tendons = active ligaments (stabilize and mobilize). Exams swap them.

The PDF writes "concave and concave" for the saddle joint — an obvious typo for the reciprocal concave–convex fit of a rider on a saddle.

Memory trick — count the axes 3 = ball (spheroid) · 2 = egg and saddle (ellipsoid, sellar) · 1 = pulley and pivot (ginglymus, cylindrical) · plane = just slides.

5. Adapting structures p. 3–4 2024-25 · 2025-26

Definition Intra-articular fibrocartilaginous structures interposed between non-congruent articular surfaces to ensure optimal alignment and congruence. So they exist only where the surfaces don't fit, not in every joint.
TypeDescriptionExample
MeniscusMobile fibrocartilaginous crescentic plateKnee menisci

6. Joint mechanics p. 4 2023-24

The primary function of a synovial joint is movement. Each movement occurs around a specific axis, within a defined plane; joint mobility ranges from 0° to 180°.

PairDefinitionRelated movements
Flexion – extensionFlexion: brings a part forward / decreases the angle between segments.
Extension: moves it backward / increases the angle.		Dorsiflexion and plantarflexion (talocrural joint)
Abduction – adductionAbduction: away from the median plane.
Adduction: toward the median plane.		 Radial and ulnar deviation / inclination (radiocarpal joint)
External – internal rotationExternal (lateral): directs the part outward (medial → lateral).
Internal (medial): inward (lateral → medial).		Pronation and supination (forearm)
Circumduction A composite, circular movement: the sequential combination of four primary movements — adduction, abduction, flexion and extension. It is not a rotation.

Exam traps — the wrong options past exams used

False statements to recognise
  • "Ligaments are the active stabilizers" / "tendons of periarticular muscles are passive stabilizers" — false: ligaments are passive, tendons are the active ligaments. 2024-25
  • "Hyaline cartilage has the same thickness in all joints" — false (key). 2024-25 · 2025-26
  • Ginglymus joints "unite a concave and a convex surface" (that's the ellipsoid) / "are condylar" / "are amphiarthroses" — false: pulley-shaped surfaces, 1 axis, a diarthrosis. 2023-24
  • "Menisci are fibrocartilages of the elbow" — false: of the knee. 2024-25
  • Adapting structures "exist in all joints" / "lie between the capsule and the synovium" — false: only between non-congruent articular surfaces, inside the joint. 2025-26
  • Adduction "is specific to spheroid joints" / "carries the anterior surface outward" — false: adduction moves the part toward the median plane; outward rotation is external rotation. 2023-24
  • "Synarthroses are temporary joints" / "their surfaces are covered with articular cartilage" — false (keys): they are fibrous, immovable. 2024-25
Asked by past exams but not in this year's handout The official keys also used these facts, which this year's PDF no longer teaches: the synovial membrane is richly vascularized and innervated; articular cartilage is not vascularized, its lesions are not reversible and its degeneration causes osteoarthritis; adapting fibrocartilages have no vessels or nerves (so a tear doesn't heal) and are essential for joint stability; synarthroses subtypes — sutures = synfibroses, tooth–socket = gomphosis, syndesmoses join distant surfaces; adduction occurs in the frontal plane.

Self-check

What can a joint connect? Give an example of each.
Two bones (sacroiliac), two cartilages (larynx), a bone and a cartilage (sternochondral).
Name the 3 joint types with their tissue and mobility.
Synarthrosis: fibrous, immovable. Amphiarthrosis: cartilaginous, slightly movable (midline). Diarthrosis: synovial, freely movable.
Is the distal tibiofibular joint an amphiarthrosis?
No — a synarthrosis (fibrous), like interosseous membranes, sutures and the fused hip bone.
What are the 4 principal structures of a diarthrosis?
Articular surfaces, joint cavity, retaining structures (capsule, passive ligaments, active ligaments), synovial membrane.
What covers the articular surfaces, and where are they located?
Smooth hyaline cartilage; at the epiphyses of long bones or the angles/surfaces of flat bones.
What are active ligaments?
The tendons of muscles adjacent to the joint; they stabilize and actively mobilize it. Passive ligaments only reinforce the capsule.
Where does the synovial membrane attach, and what are the 3 functions of synovia?
Lines the inside of the capsule, attaches at the margins of the cartilages. Waste resorption, nutrition of the avascular cartilage, lubrication.
Which diarthroses are biaxial? Which are uniaxial?
Biaxial: ellipsoid (radiocarpal), saddle (trapeziometacarpal). Uniaxial: ginglymus (humeroulnar), cylindrical (proximal radioulnar).
What type is the acromioclavicular joint, and how does it move?
Plane joint: limited gliding in multiple directions.
Meniscus, labrum, disc — which is fixed, which mobile, which complete?
Meniscus = mobile crescent (knee); labrum = fixed ring that deepens a socket (glenoid); disc = complete partition (sternoclavicular).
What does extension do to the angle between segments?
Increases it (flexion decreases it).
Pronation/supination and radial/ulnar deviation are related to which fundamental movements?
Pronation/supination → rotation (forearm); radial/ulnar deviation → abduction/adduction (radiocarpal).
What is circumduction?
A composite circular movement combining adduction, abduction, flexion and extension in sequence.
