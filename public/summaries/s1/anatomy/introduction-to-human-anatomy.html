If you remember only this
  • Anatomy comes from Greek anatomia / anatome (Latin anatomia) = "to cut through" / "to dissect" (ana = from bottom to top, tomein = to cut).
  • Descriptive anatomy = analytical study of the morphology of separate organs → the basis of anatomy.
  • Topographic (regional) anatomy = location and relations of organs within one region → the basis of surgery.
  • Ibn Al-Haitham (965–1040): first to describe the anatomy of the eye, founder of modern optics, pioneer of the scientific method.
  • Vesalius: De Humani Corporis Fabrica (1543), first public dissection; for him anatomy is "the only solid basis of the whole art of medicine".
  • Aristotle: first to use the word "anatomy", founder of comparative anatomy. Ibn an-Nafis: first description of pulmonary circulation. Morgagni: founder of pathological anatomy. Röntgen: X-rays (1895).
  • Anthropological anatomy = morphological variations in human races; artistic anatomy = external forms, for artists.

1. Definitions and importance p. 3 slides p. 10–12

Anatomy An ancient science (back to antiquity) that studies the parts making up living organisms — their form, dimensions, relationships, structure — by dissection or any other method of investigation.
AuthorDefinition
Etymology (Greek)anatomia / anatome = "to cut through", "to dissect". Ana = from bottom to top; tomein = to cut.
Paturet (1951)"Science whose object is the study of the constitution of organized beings."
Rouvière"Science of body structures."
Ribet (1961)"Science whose object is the study of the form, the reciprocal relationships and the final structure of the organs of organized beings, among them man."

2. History of anatomy p. 3–4 slides p. 13–38 2023-24

2.1 The beginnings

Who / whenContribution
3000 BC (Egypt)First anatomical descriptions on papyrus (heart, blood, liver…); mummification shows their anatomical knowledge (slide: mummy of Seti I).
Hippocrates (460–377 BC)Taught human anatomy in Greece.
Aristotle (384–322 BC)Used the term "anatomy" ("anatome") for the first time; founder of comparative anatomy.
Claudius Galen (131–201 AD, Pergamon), "prince of physicians"Taught anatomy and wrote on it; research on monkeys (slides: also pigs and other animals); wrote on anatomical practice in 15 books.

2.2 Anatomy among Muslims (10th–13th century) 2023-24

WhoContribution
Ibn Al-Haitham (965 Basra – 1040 Cairo)First to describe the anatomy of the eye; research on lenses; pioneer of the scientific method and founder of modern optics.
Ibn Sina (Avicenna) (980–1037, Uzbekistan)Dissected corpses from the battlefields; wrote the "Canon of Medicine" (Kitab Al-Qanun fi Al-Tibb).
Ibn Rushd (Averroes) (1126–1198)Wrote "Anatomy of the Organs"; (slides) commentaries on Ibn Sina's Canon.
Ibn an-Nafis (1213–1288)First description of pulmonary circulation; wrote "Comments on the anatomy of Ibn Sina's Canon".

2.3 Renaissance (15th–16th century)

WhoContribution
Jacques Dubois (Sylvius) (1478–1555)Notes: described the middle cerebral artery. Slides: described the quadratus plantae (accessory muscle of the long flexor, "Sylvius's square flesh").
Gabriel Fallopius (1523–1562)Described the uterine tube and the facial nerve (canal).
Andreas Vesalius (1514–1564)Professor of anatomy; reference work De Humani Corporis Fabrica, 1543; first public dissection.

2.4 17th century

  • William Harvey (1578–1657): rediscovered and demonstrated pulmonary circulation.
  • Malpighi (1628–1684): discovered capillary vessels; (slides) creator of microscopic anatomy.
  • Steno: demonstrated cardiac muscle contraction.
  • Aselli, Pecquet (1622–1674): discovered and demonstrated lymphatic circulation.

2.5 18th century

  • Birth of comparative anatomy, anthropology and biology.
  • Morgagni (1682–1771): founded pathological anatomy.
  • (Slides) Xavier Bichat (1771–1802): created general anatomy (study of tissues); book (1801) "General anatomy applied to physiology and medicine".
  • Dissection amphitheatres and natural history galleries appear throughout Europe.

2.6 19th century

  • Period of histology: cell theory demonstrated.
  • Development of topographic and surgical anatomy, for the safety of amputations.
  • Corti, Pacini, Golgi: neuroanatomy.
  • 1819–1899: tools to study anatomy on the living — stethoscope, otoscope, ophthalmoscope, laryngoscope, gastroscope, cystoscope, bronchoscope.
  • Around 1890: formaldehyde (formol) used as a fixative.
  • Conrad Röntgen (1845–1923): discovered X-rays in 1895 (first radiograph: his wife's hand, 8 November 1895).

2.7 20th century and today

  • (Slides) Sectional anatomy by CT scan and MRI; functional anatomy by dynamic MRI, ultrasound, echocardiography.
  • 1993 — Visible Human Project: a 3D virtual human showing real human anatomy after dissection. Notes: a condemned man cut into 1800 thin slices. Slides: two cadavers (a man and a woman) cut into thin slices, each photographed and digitized; run by the US National Library of Medicine (Michael J. Ackerman).
  • Anatomy is now mainly applied: medicine, surgery, imaging, teaching (and anatomo-clinical research).

3. Branches of anatomy (12) p. 4–5 slides p. 39–67 2024-25 · 2025-26

#BranchWhat it studies
1Descriptive (explanatory) 🎯Analytical study of the morphology of separate organs: shape, dimensions, weight, colour, consistency, constitution, internal structure. The basic study in anatomy.
2Topographic (regional) 🎯Location and relationships of organs within the same anatomical region. The basic study of surgery.
3FunctionalFunction of organs and its relation to morphology.
4SystemicThe body's systems and organs (apparatuses).
5Radiological (imaging)Morphology by imaging: X-ray, CT scan, MRI, scintigraphy…
6Medical-surgical (clinical, applied)Anatomy applied to the clinic.
7ComparativeRelations between homologous structures of all animals including humans — between individuals (ontogeny) or between species (phylogeny).
8AnthropologicalMorphological variations in human races.
9DevelopmentalMorphological transformations from fertilization to adulthood: prenatal (embryology, fetology), postnatal (growth), teratology (congenital malformations).
10PathologicalAlterations of anatomical structures caused by diseases.
11Microscopic (structural)Microscopic structure of cells (cytology) and tissues (histology).
12Artistic (plastic, of forms)External forms of the body, for sculptors, engravers and painters (slide: Leonardo da Vinci's hand).
Memory trick Descriptive = Detached organs, basis of anatomy. Topographic = Territory (a region) and its neighbours, basis of surgery — the surgeon needs to know what lies next to what.

Exam traps — the wrong options past exams used

False statements to recognise
  • "Descriptive anatomy = the study of the location / the relations of organs" — false: that is topographic anatomy. Descriptive = morphology of separate organs. 2 years
  • "Descriptive anatomy is the basis of surgery" / "topographic anatomy is the basis of anatomy" — false, it's the other way round. 2 years
  • "Topographic anatomy = morphological variations in human races" — false: that is anthropological anatomy. 2025-26
  • "Ibn Sina / Ibn Nafis / Ibn Rushd / Al-Zahrawi founded modern optics and first described the eye" — false: it is Ibn Al-Haitham. 2023-24
  • Quotes swapped between authors: "the only solid basis of the whole art of medicine" is Vesalius, not Paturet; "science of external forms" is artistic anatomy, not Rouvière (Rouvière = science of body structures).

Self-check — answer, then tap to reveal

Where does the word "anatomy" come from and what does it mean?
Greek anatomia / anatome, Latin anatomia: "to cut through", "to dissect" (ana = from bottom to top, tomein = to cut).
Give the definitions of Paturet, Rouvière and Ribet.
Paturet (1951): study of the constitution of organized beings. Rouvière: science of body structures. Ribet (1961): study of the form, reciprocal relationships and final structure of the organs of organized beings, including man.
Who said anatomy is "the only solid basis of the whole art of medicine", and when?
Vesalius, 1543.
Who first used the word "anatomy" and founded comparative anatomy?
Aristotle (384–322 BC).
Match: Ibn Al-Haitham, Ibn Sina, Ibn Rushd, Ibn an-Nafis.
Ibn Al-Haitham: anatomy of the eye, modern optics. Ibn Sina: battlefield corpses, Canon of Medicine. Ibn Rushd: Anatomy of the Organs. Ibn an-Nafis: first description of pulmonary circulation.
What did Vesalius do?
Professor of anatomy; wrote De Humani Corporis Fabrica (1543); performed the first public dissection.
Harvey, Malpighi, Pecquet, Morgagni, Röntgen — one discovery each.
Harvey: rediscovered/demonstrated pulmonary circulation. Malpighi: capillaries. Pecquet (with Aselli): lymphatic circulation. Morgagni: founded pathological anatomy. Röntgen: X-rays, 1895.
Why did topographic and surgical anatomy develop in the 19th century?
For the safety of amputations.
What is the Visible Human Project?
1993: a 3D virtual human built from a body (the slides say a man and a woman) cut into thin slices (1800 in the notes), photographed and digitized, showing real human anatomy.
Descriptive vs topographic anatomy: definition and what each is the basis of.
Descriptive: morphology of separate organs → basis of anatomy. Topographic: location and relations of organs within a region → basis of surgery.
Which branch studies variations between human races? Which one external forms for artists?
Anthropological anatomy; artistic (plastic) anatomy.
What does developmental anatomy cover?
Fertilization to adulthood: prenatal development (embryology, fetology), postnatal development (growth), teratology (congenital malformations).
Ontogeny vs phylogeny in comparative anatomy?
Ontogeny: comparison between individuals. Phylogeny: comparison between species.
Name the study methods of anatomy.
Dissection (the basic technique), plastination, imaging, endoscopy, surgery, microscopy.
