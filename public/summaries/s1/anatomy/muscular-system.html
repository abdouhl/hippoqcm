If you remember only this
  • Muscle = organ that contracts (shortens) under a stimulus (nervous, electrical, thermal, painful…); studied by myology.
  • Roles: movement, heat (thermogenesis), posture, joint stabilization, protection of viscera, visceral functions.
  • Skeletal = striped/red, striated, voluntary, fast, fatigues fast, CNS · Cardiac = mixed, striated, involuntary/automatic/rhythmic, never fatigues, ANS · Visceral = smooth, non-striated, involuntary/slow/continuous, never fatigues, ANS.
  • Deep = sub-aponeurotic (digestive/respiratory systems, sensory organs) · superficial = supra-aponeurotic, subcutaneous (facial muscles).
  • Varieties: digastric (2 bellies in series), multigastric (rectus abdominis), bifid (biceps brachii), multifidus (quadriceps); parallel tendons = heads.
  • Insertion sites: bone (iliopsoas), cartilage (cricothyroid), aponeurosis (abductor hallucis), skin (palmaris brevis).
  • 4 appendices: serous bursae, tendinous serous sheaths, fibrous sheaths (osteofibrous canal), fascia = covering aponeurosis (encases and separates muscles).

I. Introduction p. 1 2023-24

Muscle The driving force of the locomotor system: an organ that contracts (shortens) under the influence of a stimulus — nervous, electrical, thermal, painful excitation, etc. The study of muscles is myology.

Roles of muscles (6):

  • Production of movement
  • Heat production (thermogenesis)
  • Maintaining posture
  • Joint stabilization
  • Protection of the viscera
  • Visceral functions: digestion, defecation, urination, blood propulsion, vasoconstriction, vasodilation, childbirth, etc.

The PDF writes "Ship walls" — a mistranslation of vessel walls (French "vaisseaux").

Memory trick Cardiac muscle is "mixed" because it sits between the other two: striated like skeletal, but involuntary and ANS-controlled like visceral.

III.2 Division p. 2

A simple (monogastric) muscle has 3 parts:

  • A middle section, the muscular belly (body): large, red, fleshy, contractile.
  • Two ends, the tendons: narrow, whitish, elastic, resistant.
VarietyBelliesExample
Digastric2 bellies + an intermediate tendon (in series)Digastric muscle
MultigastricSeveral bellies + several intermediate tendons (in series)Rectus abdominis
Bifid2 bellies in parallelBiceps brachii
MultifidusSeveral bellies in parallelQuadriceps femoris

In bifid and multifid muscles, the parallel tendons are called heads.

Don't mix up Multigastric = bellies one after the other (rectus abdominis). Multifidus = bellies side by side (quadriceps).

III.3 Insertion p. 2 2025-26

A muscle has a double insertion:

  • Origin: proximal or fixed.
  • Termination: distal or mobile.

Note: the origin can sometimes become mobile.

Anatomical structures useful to the muscle, facilitating its action. There are four:

Exam traps — the wrong options past exams used

False statements to recognise
  • Roles of muscles include "vitamin production" / "concentration of clotting factors" — false. 2023-24
  • Striated muscles "are visceral muscles" — false: skeletal muscles, attached to the skeleton. 2024-25
  • Smooth muscles "are under voluntary control" / "are located in the arm, the neck" — false: ANS, viscera. 2024-25
  • The sub-aponeurotic muscle "is subcutaneous" / "lies between the fascia and the skin" / "is involuntary with rapid contractions" — false: it is deep (e.g. pharynx and larynx muscles); subcutaneous = superficial. 2025-26
  • "Attaches to the fascia, like the cricothyroid" / "inserts onto cartilage, like the abductor hallucis" — false: cricothyroid = cartilage, abductor hallucis = aponeurosis. 2025-26
  • The muscular fascia "is a fibrous groove delimiting osteofibrous canals" (that's the fibrous sheath) / "facilitates muscle gliding" (that's the serous bursa) — false. 2025-26
Asked by past exams but not in this year's handout
  • 2023-24 asked the properties of muscle: tonicity, elasticity, contractility, excitability (not "rigidity").
  • 2024-25 described striated muscle as made of elongated cells (key).
  • ⚠️ 2024-25 Q25 "mixed muscles" keys myocardium, middle-ear muscles and diaphragm. This year's handout calls only cardiac muscle "mixed" — ask your teacher which definition the exam will use.

Self-check

Define muscle. Which science studies it?
An organ that contracts (shortens) under a stimulus (nervous, electrical, thermal, painful…), driving force of the locomotor system. Myology.
List the 6 roles of muscles.
Movement, thermogenesis, posture, joint stabilization, protection of the viscera, visceral functions (digestion, urination, blood propulsion, childbirth…).
Which muscle types have striated cells? Which never fatigue?
Striated: skeletal and cardiac. Never fatigue: cardiac and visceral.
Which nervous system controls each muscle type?
Skeletal: CNS. Cardiac and visceral: ANS.
Describe the contraction of visceral muscle.
Involuntary, slow, continuous.
Are facial muscles deep or superficial?
Superficial: supra-aponeurotic, subcutaneous.
Describe the belly and tendons of a monogastric muscle.
Belly: large, red, fleshy, contractile. Tendons: narrow, whitish, elastic, resistant.
Rectus abdominis, biceps brachii, quadriceps femoris — which variety is each?
Multigastric; bifid; multifidus.
Is the origin of a muscle always fixed?
No — it is usually proximal/fixed but can sometimes become mobile.
Give the 4 insertion sites with an example each.
Bone (iliopsoas), cartilage (cricothyroid), aponeurosis (abductor hallucis), skin (palmaris brevis).
Give an example of a flat, a short and an annular muscle.
Latissimus dorsi; hypothenar muscles; orbicularis oculi / oris.
What is the difference between a fibrous sheath and a tendinous serous sheath?
Fibrous sheath: fibrous groove forming an osteofibrous canal with the bone. Serous sheath: serous bursa wrapped around the tendon, sliding inside that canal.
What is the other name of the muscular fascia, and what does it do?
Covering aponeurosis; encases and separates the muscles.
