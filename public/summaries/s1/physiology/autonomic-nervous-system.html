If you remember only this
  • The ANS regulates cardiac muscle, smooth muscle and glands; mostly involuntary but its control centres lie in the CNS ("autonomic" = automatic, not autonomous).
  • Sympathetic: thoracolumbar (T1–L2, lateral horn), ganglia far from the organ → short pre, long post, high divergence, "fight or flight". Parasympathetic: craniosacral (CN III, VII, IX, X + S2–S4), ganglia near/in the organ → long pre, short post, "rest and digest".
  • Every ganglion: ACh → Nn (both divisions). Target: parasympathetic ACh → muscarinic; most sympathetic NE → α/β.
  • Sweat glands = the cholinergic exception of the sympathetic system (ACh → M₃). Adrenal medulla = modified sympathetic ganglion, no postganglionic neuron: ACh → Nn → adrenaline ~80 %, NE ~20 % into the blood.
  • Gs ↑ cAMP (β) · Gi ↓ cAMP (α₂, M₂) · Gq ↑ Ca²⁺ (α₁, M₃). Presynaptic α₂ reduce NE release.
  • Heart: sympathetic ↑ (β₁), parasympathetic ↓ (M₂). Liver: ↑ glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis (sympathetic). Saliva: parasympathetic abundant watery, sympathetic scant.
  • Hierarchy: cortex/limbic → hypothalamus (main coordinator) → brainstem (vital reflexes) → spinal cord (bladder, bowel).

1. Overview and homeostasis p. 2–3 2022-23

Autonomic nervous system A functional division of the nervous system that regulates visceral effectors: cardiac muscle, smooth muscle and glands (blood vessels are major targets because their walls contain smooth muscle). Classified in the peripheral nervous system, although its control centres lie in the CNS. Predominantly involuntary, but changed by emotions, exercise, sleep and breathing.
FeatureSomatic motor systemAutonomic motor system
TargetsSkeletal muscleCardiac muscle, smooth muscle, glands
Efferent pathwayOne neuron CNS → muscleUsually two neurons, relayed in a peripheral ganglion
Transmitter at targetACh on nicotinic muscle receptors (Nm)ACh or NE, depending on the pathway
Effect on targetAlways excitatoryExcitatory or inhibitory
ControlVoluntary movement and reflexesPredominantly involuntary

Visceral sensory (afferent) neurons report internal conditions (stretch, pressure, chemistry); autonomic motor (efferent) pathways carry the commands. Four homeostatic jobs: cardiovascular (heart rate, vessel tone), digestive (motility, secretion), thermoregulation (sweating, skin blood flow), bladder (storage and emptying). Homeostasis is continuous adjustment, not a fixed state.

2. Organization of autonomic pathways p. 3–5 2022-23 · 2023-24

CNS (brainstem or spinal cord)preganglionic neuronautonomic ganglionpostganglionic neuroneffector

A ganglion is a cluster of neuronal cell bodies outside the CNS. Three components of the ANS:

  • Sympathetic: thoracolumbar; mobilizes for activity and stress ("fight or flight").
  • Parasympathetic: craniosacral; digestion, secretion, elimination, recovery ("rest and digest").
  • Enteric nervous system: intrinsic network in the gut wall; can organize local activity on its own, modulated by both divisions.

Sympathetic outflow (thoracolumbar)

Preganglionic neurons in the lateral horn of the spinal cord, T1–L2. Three routes:

Adrenal medulla 2022-23 Behaves like a modified sympathetic ganglion: the preganglionic fibre reaches it directly; its chromaffin cells (modified postganglionic neurons) are stimulated by preganglionic ACh through Nn receptors and release hormones into the blood instead of sending an axon to an organ.

Parasympathetic outflow (craniosacral)

Preganglionic neurons in the brainstem (CN III, VII, IX, X) and sacral cord (S2–S4); ganglia close to or inside the target → very local control.

3. Neurotransmitters and receptors p. 6–7 2022-23 · 2023-24 · 2024-25

Two rules cover almost every case
  1. In every autonomic ganglion, the preganglionic neuron releases ACh on neuronal nicotinic (Nn) receptors — both divisions.
  2. At the target: parasympathetic fibres release ACh (muscarinic receptors); most sympathetic fibres release NE (adrenergic α or β).
Exceptions and special cases
  1. Eccrine sweat glands: sympathetic but cholinergic — postganglionic ACh onto M₃. The key exception.
  2. Adrenal medulla: no postganglionic neuron; ACh → Nn on chromaffin cells → adrenaline (~80 %) + NE (~20 %) into the blood.
  3. Kidney (β₁ → renin) and adipose tissue (β₃ → lipolysis) are not exceptions: normal NE, particular receptor subtype.
  4. Bronchi and some vascular beds depend mainly on circulating adrenaline on β₂ (little direct sympathetic innervation).
Sympathetic cholinergic vasodilator fibres to skeletal muscle exist in some animals; their role in humans is debated.

Memory aid (from the handout) Gs = Stimulates (↑ cAMP) · Gi = Inhibits (↓ cAMP) · Gq = Ca²⁺ (IP₃/DAG).

The effect also depends on the tissue: M₃ contracts the iris sphincter, but endothelial M₃ relaxes vascular smooth muscle by releasing NO.

4. Visceral reflexes and central control p. 7–8 2022-23

Receptor detects a change → sensory pathway → integrating circuit → motor response in an effector.

ExampleStretch of the gut wall → nearby contractionBladder filling → urination

Central regulation — a hierarchy of centres (why exam stress speeds the heart and slows digestion):

  1. Cortex and limbic system: emotions, thoughts, behaviour.
  2. Hypothalamus: main coordinator of autonomic responses and homeostasis (temperature, hunger, thirst).
  3. Brainstem: vital reflexes — heart rate, blood pressure, breathing; the vagus nerve is its direct route to heart, lungs and gut.
  4. Spinal cord: reflexes for bladder and bowel emptying.

Exam traps — the wrong options past exams used

False statements to recognise
  • The ANS regulatory centre "is peripheral" / "does not exist, it is autonomous" — false: it is central. 2022-23
  • Dry mouth is due to "cholinergic stimulation" — false: cholinergic inhibition and/or adrenergic stimulation (scant saliva). 2022-23
  • Muscarinic receptors are "in autonomic ganglia" (those are Nn) or "in skeletal muscle" (Nm) — false; they are in the gut and bronchial smooth muscle. 2022-23 · 2023-24
  • The adrenal medulla "is a parasympathetic ganglion" — false: a modified sympathetic ganglion. 2022-23
  • In the liver, adrenergic stimulation causes "glycogenesis" or "lipolysis" — false: glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis (lipolysis is adipose, β₃). 2023-24
  • Sympathetic postganglionic fibres to sweat glands are "adrenergic" — false: cholinergic (pre short and cholinergic too). 2023-24
  • Presynaptic α₂ "cause vasoconstriction" (that's α₁) / "are inhibited by ACh" — false: excess NE stimulates them → negative feedback on NE release. 2023-24
  • Parasympathetic postganglionic neurons release "NE on α/β", "NE on nicotinic" or "ACh on adrenergic receptors" — false: ACh on muscarinic. 2024-25
Asked by past exams but not in this handout The official keys also say: the adrenal medulla has a catabolic effect and releases small amounts of adrenaline/NE at rest (2022-23); muscarinic receptors are stimulated by organophosphates and inhibited by atropine (2023-24).

Self-check — answer, then tap to reveal

Which effectors does the ANS regulate?
Cardiac muscle, smooth muscle and glands (vessels through their smooth muscle).
Somatic vs autonomic: number of neurons and effect on target?
Somatic: one neuron, always excitatory (ACh → Nm). Autonomic: usually two neurons, excitatory or inhibitory.
Origins of the two divisions?
Sympathetic: T1–L2 lateral horn. Parasympathetic: CN III, VII, IX, X and S2–S4.
Which division has short preganglionic fibres and high divergence?
The sympathetic.
Which ganglion supplies the parotid? The lacrimal glands?
Otic (CN IX); pterygopalatine (CN VII).
Transmitter and receptor in every autonomic ganglion?
ACh on neuronal nicotinic (Nn) receptors, in both divisions.
What makes sweat glands an exception?
Sympathetic but cholinergic: postganglionic ACh on M₃.
What does the adrenal medulla release, in what proportion?
Adrenaline ~80 % and noradrenaline ~20 %, into the blood (ACh → Nn on chromaffin cells).
Which G protein for α₁, α₂, β and M₂?
α₁: Gq · α₂: Gi/o · β: Gs · M₂: Gi/o (+ K⁺ channels).
Role of presynaptic α₂ receptors?
They reduce NE release (negative feedback).
Short vs long reflex?
Short: enteric plexus/ganglion, no CNS, local. Long: through spinal cord or brainstem, can coordinate organs (urination).
Main coordinator of autonomic responses?
The hypothalamus.
Sympathetic effects on liver, pancreas and kidney?
Liver: ↑ glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis. Pancreas: ↓ insulin (α₂). Kidney: ↑ renin (β₁).
Who controls bladder storage and emptying?
Storage: sympathetic (detrusor relaxes β₃, sphincter contracts α₁). Emptying: parasympathetic (detrusor contracts, M₃).
Give one opposing and one complementary effect.
Opposing: heart rate, pupil, bladder. Complementary: salivation, sexual function.
