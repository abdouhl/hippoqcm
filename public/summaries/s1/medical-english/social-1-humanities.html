If you remember only this
  • Both fields study human life (behaviour and interaction in social, cultural, environmental, economic, political contexts). Humanities = mostly analytical / philosophical; social sciences = more scientific / experimental.
  • Humanities (the liberal arts): from the ancient Greeks; modern conception from the Sophists, mid-5th century BCE (citizenship in the polis). Non-science disciplines: history, philosophy, religion, languages & literatures, arts, media, cultural studies.
  • Humanities: creative and speculative methods, narrative imagination, subjective, no search for objective truth, no causal explanation; self-reflexive — "what it means to be human".
  • Social science: scientific study of social, cultural, psychological, economic and political forces guiding individuals; systematic, objective (facts, experiments, reality).
  • Man studied in 2 dimensions: individual being (e.g. psychology) and social being (e.g. sociology).

1–2. Humanities p. 1–2

  • Branches of knowledge concerned with human thoughts, creative expressions and culture; they focus on meaning, purpose and goals, and the appreciation of singular historical and social phenomena; how humans construct a world of meanings and interpretations.
  • Encyclopedia Britannica (2006): "Branches of knowledge that investigate human beings, their culture and their self-expression" (philosophy, literature, languages, arts, religion, history). Disciplines studying the human condition in totality = Humanities or Human Sciences.
  • Expressing oneself through the arts is "one of the earliest and most basic of human activities". Emphasis on analysis and exchange of ideas; they help decide what is important, identify right and wrong, understand heritage and history.
  • Core of a well-rounded education since ancient times; encourage creative and critical thinking, reasoning and asking questions.
  • What distinguishes them from natural sciences = the mode of approach: they neither seek objective truth nor explain the causality of events.

3. Similarities and differences p. 3

Similarity: both deal with human beings, cultures and societies. Humanities treat them in terms of philosophical and spiritual aspects — how human experience is processed and documented (literature, music, art, language, philosophy, religion, history).

Social sciencesHumanities
Basic elements of culture that determine the general patterns of human behaviourSpecial aspects of culture expressing spiritual and aesthetic values, the meaning of life
Study issues in a systematic and scientific wayFocus more on emotions and feelings than on the system employed
More objective: facts, experiments, realityMore subjective: experience, feelings, assumptions, philosophy

4. Social sciences as an academic discipline p. 3–4

Concerned with society: understand all its aspects and find solutions to social problems; how people interact, behave, develop a culture and influence the world.

ScholarDefinition of social sciences
SeligmanMental or cultural sciences dealing with the activities of the individual as member of the group — the relationship of man to society
MitchellLoosely, any study of man and society; strictly, the application of scientific method to the complex network of human relationships and organization
FairchildGeneral term for all sciences concerned with human affairs
Peter LewisConcerned with the laws that govern society, the social department of man
Dimension of "man"MeaningExample
Individual beingThe human being as an isolated item (way of thinking)Psychology
Social beingThe human being within society (behaviour with others)Sociology

5. Importance in foreign-language learning p. 4–5

  • Language is a means through which learners interact and the subject of their study; FL learning develops cognitive, social and linguistic competence at the same time.
  • Knowing other societies, cultures and people → deeper understanding of the target language → effective, appropriate communication and confidence.
  • Social-science thinking underlies law, international relations and government → needed to understand current world events.
  • English = the first international medium of communication; essential in education, digital/internet communication, the professional world and for studying abroad.
