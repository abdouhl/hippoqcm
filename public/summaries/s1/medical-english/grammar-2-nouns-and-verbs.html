If you remember only this
  • Uncountable: pain, information, equipment, blood, fever, treatment (concept) → one form, singular verb, no a/an or numbers, much. Countable: symptom, diagnosis, patient, test, infection → many.
  • Illness names are usually uncountable: "Measles is…", flu, diabetes, pneumonia; minor ailments countable: a cold, a sore throat, a headache.
  • Plurals: -a→-ae · -us→-i · -um→-a · -is→-es · -ex/-ix→-ices · -on→-a · consonant+y→-ies. "Bacterias" ✗.
  • Collocations: make a diagnosis · take a temperature/history · run a test · perform surgery/an examination · administer an injection · receive treatment · undergo surgery.
  • Nominalisation sounds more formal: "The patient made a good recovery" > "recovered well".

3.1–3.2 High-frequency medical verbs p. 5–6

Choose a precise verb rather than a vague "do" or "make".

CategoryVerbs
Examination & assessmentexamine, assess, evaluate, investigate, test, monitor, observe
Diagnosisdiagnose, identify, detect, confirm, rule out
Treatment & caretreat, prescribe, administer, perform, operate, manage, care for
Patient statespresent (with), suffer (from), recover, relapse, respond, improve, deteriorate
Clinical actionsrefer, admit, discharge, document, report, record

Tenses: present simple = current condition ("presents with chest pain") · present perfect = past action with present relevance ("has been prescribed") · past simple = completed past action ("was diagnosed in 2019").

5. Noun–verb pairs p. 5, 8–9

  • Verb → noun: examine → examination · diagnose → diagnosis · treat → treatment · prescribe → prescription · administer → administration · investigate → investigation · monitor → monitoring · recover → recovery (suffixes -ment, -ion, -ing).
  • Noun → verb: pain → to pain · discharge → discharge · referral → refer ("The GP referred the patient to cardiology").
  • Nominalisation = more objective, professional: "The patient made a good recovery" (more formal) vs "recovered well".

Common errors — from the lecture and its formative assessment

Wrong → right
  • The doctor did a diagnosis → made · made an examination → performed/conducted
  • The patient took treatment → received/underwent · got surgery → underwent
  • The nurse gave medication → administered (more formal, precise) · give a test → run a test
  • many pain → much pain · several bacterias → bacteria · Measles are → Measles is
  • "medicaments" → medication(s) (medicament is rarely used in modern medical English)
  • "The patient answered/replied well to treatment" → responded

What are delexical verbs and why must collocations be learned whole?
General verbs (make, take, run…) paired with specific nouns; the combinations can't be predicted from translation.
Which tense for "a past action with present relevance"?
Present perfect: "She has been prescribed metformin."
Make "The patient recovered well" more formal.
"The patient made a good recovery." (nominalisation)
