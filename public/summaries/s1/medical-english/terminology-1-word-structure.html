If you remember only this
  • Medical terms = root (core meaning: body part, system, function — cardi- = heart) + prefix (beginning: time, location, number, status — hyper- = above/excessive) + suffix (end: condition, disease, procedure, grammatical form — -itis = inflammation, -logy = study of).
  • A combining vowel, usually "o", links elements to aid pronunciation: gastr-o-enter-o-logy (stomach + intestine + study).
  • Procedure suffixes: -ostomy new opening · -otomy incision · -ectomy removal · -oscopy viewing · -centesis puncture · -plasty repair · -otripsy crush · -desis fuse · -scope instrument · -opsy inspection.
  • Suffixes change the part of speech: arthritis (noun) → arthritic (adjective); diagnose (verb) → diagnosis (noun).

2. The three components p. 3

PartPositionRoleExample
RootCoreEssential meaning, often a body part, system or functioncardi- = heart
PrefixBeginningModifies the meaning: time, location, number, statushyper- = above, excessive · hypo- = below
SuffixEndCondition, disease, procedure or grammatical form-itis = inflammation · -logy = study of

cardi- + -logy = cardiology, "the study of the heart". Task example: neuro- (nerve) + -pathy (disease) = neuropathy. Breaking terms down lets you deduce meanings accurately.

Self-check — answer, then tap to reveal

What kinds of information does a prefix add?
Time, location, number or status.
What is the usual combining vowel, and why is it used?
"o" — to aid pronunciation (gastroenterology).
-ostomy, -otomy, -ectomy?
Create a new opening · cut into · remove.
What do -centesis and -desis mean?
Puncture/aspiration · bind/fuse together.
Reno-, rhino-, tympano-, vesico-?
Kidney · nose · eardrum · bladder/blister.

Infection · infectious.
