If you remember only this
  • Reading medical texts = selective cognitive process; key terms are almost always content words (especially nouns) — medical English is a nominalized register.
  • 5 types: pathological (anemia), anatomical (femur), procedural (dialysis), diagnostic (MRI, biopsy), pharmacological (insulin).
  • Skimming = global understanding (topic); scanning = specific information (a value, a diagnosis).
  • 6-step extraction: title/headings → repetition → nouns & noun phrases → remove function words → group → validate in context.
  • Priority: high = diagnoses/diseases/outcomes · medium = procedures/explanations · low = supporting descriptions.

1–2. Key terms and content words p. 2–4

  • Key term identification supports lecture comprehension, note-taking, exam preparation, scientific reading accuracy and clinical interpretation; it reduces cognitive overload in texts of high lexical density.
  • Medical discourse is dominated by nouns and nominal compounds; meaning sits in lexical items rather than grammar. "The patient is diagnosed" → "diagnosis" (nominalized register).
Content wordsFunction words
FunctionCarry meaning — medical conceptsGrammatical structure
Exampleshypertension, infection, diagnosis, neuronis, the, of, and, to

Dense semantic domains: diseases (diabetes, pneumonia), organs (liver, kidney, brain), procedures (biopsy, endoscopy), measurements (blood pressure, glucose level).

3. Five types of key terms p. 4

CategoryRefers toExamples
PathologicalDiseases and disordersanemia, tuberculosis, cancer
AnatomicalBody structuresheart, femur, cortex
ProceduralMedical interventionssurgery, dialysis, vaccination
DiagnosticTests and evaluationsMRI, biopsy, ECG
PharmacologicalDrugs and treatmentsantibiotics, insulin, analgesics

A clinical report mixes them: cancer (pathological) of the lung (anatomical), confirmed by biopsy (diagnostic), treated with chemotherapy (pharmacological). Grouping supports cognitive chunking.

5. Skimming and scanning p. 5–6

SkimmingScanning
PurposeGlobal understanding — topic, purpose, structureSpecific information retrieval
HowRead quickly for the general ideaSearch for predefined keywords or data
LevelMacro-level comprehensionMicro-level extraction
Example"This article discusses respiratory infections."Locate "pneumonia", "fever", "CT scan result", lab values

Together = a hierarchical reading model.

6. Keyword extraction model p. 6–7

  1. Read title and headings → identify global topic (macro-signals).
  2. Locate repeated words → conceptual importance.
  3. Identify nouns and noun phrases → extract semantic units (as whole chunks).
  4. Remove function words → filter grammatical noise.
  5. Group related terms → conceptual clusters.
  6. Validate in context → ensure accuracy, avoid misclassification.

7–8. Medical noun phrases and semantic priority p. 7–8

Noun phrases = single semantic units (modifiers + core noun specify type, severity, location, system) → decode holistically, not word by word. They increase density and precision but raise cognitive load.

Common difficulties p. 8–9

Error → cause → fix
  • Translating every word → lack of strategy → focus on key terms only.
  • Ignoring repetition → weak scanning → track repeated items.
  • Misreading noun phrases → word-by-word reading → chunking.
  • Overlooking headings → linear reading habit → structural reading.
  • Also: confusing technical and general vocabulary, over-focus on unknown words, misreading abbreviations (ECG, MRI), not separating main ideas from details.
