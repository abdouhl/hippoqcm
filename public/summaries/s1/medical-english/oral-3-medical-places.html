If you remember only this
  • Emergency Department (= A&E, Accident and Emergency / Emergency Room) → serious injuries; ambulance patients go there straight away.
  • Outpatients Department → doctors examine patients with an appointment, who wait there until called.
  • Radiology → X-rays; Pharmacy → buy/collect medicine; Operating theatre → surgery.
  • ICU is near the operating theatres (patients moved quickly after surgery); pharmacy and laboratory on the ground floor near reception (every department sends samples / collects medication).
  • Directions: next to, opposite, at the end of the corridor, on the second floor, between, past, on your left/right.

3. How a general hospital is organised (reading text) p. 2–3

  1. A central reception area, from which corridors lead to the main departments.
  2. Patients arriving by ambulance → straight to the Emergency Department.
  3. Patients with an appointment → wait in the outpatient department until called.
  4. Serious cases needing continuous monitoring → ICU, normally close to the operating theatres so patients can be moved quickly after surgery.
  5. Pharmacy and laboratory → often ground floor, near reception, since almost every department sends samples there or collects medication.

Listening: engVid, Learn MEDICAL Vocabulary in English (Adam) — basic terms such as wound, cure, heal, disease.

4. Asking for and giving directions p. 3

Model dialogue

Visitor: Excuse me, could you tell me where the radiology department is?
Staff: Of course. Go down this corridor, and it's the second door on your left, next to the pharmacy.
Visitor: Thank you. And is the waiting room nearby?
Staff: Yes, it's just opposite, on the ground floor.

Writing task: 60–80 words of directions from the main entrance to a department, using at least four prepositions of place.
