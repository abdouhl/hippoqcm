If you remember only this
  • Before any examination the patient must know who you are and what your role is.
  • A good introduction = name + role + brief explanation of what will happen next → the patient feels safe.
  • Students/trainees must say so clearly — never let the patient assume you are a fully qualified doctor or nurse.
  • Confirming full name and date of birth = politeness and a basic safety check before any procedure.
  • Ask permission: "if that's alright (with you)".

1. Key vocabulary and expressions p. 1

FunctionExpression
Name + roleI'm Dr / Nurse [name]. I'll be looking after you today.
Identity / safety checkCan you confirm your full name and date of birth, please?
Student role + permissionI'm a first-year student; I'll be observing today, if that's alright with you.
PleasantriesIt's a pleasure to meet you. / Pleased to meet you.
Putting at easePlease, make yourself comfortable.

Role nouns: physician, nurse, student nurse, consultant, receptionist, paramedic.

Warm-up: introduce yourself as a university student, as a future health professional meeting a patient, and as a patient checking in — notice how the wording changes. Listening: Preply, Episode 2: How to Introduce Yourself in English — nine key expressions.

2. Why introductions matter (reading text) p. 2

  1. The patient needs to know two things: who you are and what your role is.
  2. A short, confident introduction (name, role, what happens next) helps the patient feel safe.
  3. Students and trainees say so clearly rather than letting the patient assume they are fully qualified.
  4. Confirming name and date of birth is not just politeness: it is a basic safety check before any procedure.

3. Model dialogue p. 3

Nurse meets patient

Nurse: Good morning. My name is Nadia, and I'll be your nurse this morning.
Patient: Good morning, Nadia.
Nurse: Could you confirm your full name and date of birth for me, please?
Patient: Yes, it's Amine Cherif, the third of May, 1990.
Nurse: Thank you. I'm just going to check your blood pressure now, if that's alright.

Structure: greeting → name & role → identity check → announce the procedure + ask consent.

4. Speaking and writing tasks p. 3

  • Controlled: rewrite the dialogue changing the role (student nurse, paramedic, consultant) and the procedure.
  • Guided: a thirty-second self-introduction — name, role, one polite opening question.
  • Freer: present to the class; ask a classmate one follow-up question.
  • Writing: 70–90 words — your name, your role, one sentence on what will happen during the visit, a polite closing question.
