If you remember only this
  • Register = level of formality: "Hi there!" (informal) vs "Good morning, Madam." (formal). "How do you do?" = very formal, first meeting.
  • At reception: greet first, then ask for paperwork. Use Mr/Mrs unless the patient invites you to use their first name (more respectful).
  • Anxious patient → slow down your speech, keep a warm tone.
  • Every exchange = greeting → polite request → thanks/closing.
  • Polite request forms: Could you…? May I…? Excuse me, could I ask you something?

Listening: British Council Word on the Street, Hospital Scene 1 — Ashlie and Stephen are at the hospital, where Stephen is being examined; everyday greetings and polite requests rather than very formal language.

2. Reception etiquette (reading text) p. 2

  1. Greet first, before asking for paperwork: "Good morning, how can I help you today?" puts the person at ease.
  2. Use the patient's name once you know it, with Mr / Mrs unless they invite you to use their first name.
  3. If the patient looks anxious: slow down your speech, keep your tone warm.
  4. Close with a short, sincere phrase: "Take care, and see you at your next appointment." (alternatives: "Get well soon, and see you next week").

3. Model dialogue p. 3

At reception

Receptionist: Good morning. How can I help you?
Patient: Good morning. I have an appointment with Dr Meziane at ten o'clock.
Receptionist: Certainly. May I have your name, please?
Patient: Yes, it's Amine Larbi.
Receptionist: Thank you, Mr Larbi. Please take a seat; the doctor will call you shortly.
Patient: Thank you very much.

Checklist for any dialogue: a greeting? a polite request (Could you…, May I…)? a polite close (Thank you, Take care)? Pay attention to polite intonation on "Good morning" and "Thank you very much".

4. Writing: appointment confirmation email p. 3

  • 60–80 words, at least three polite expressions; must contain a greeting, the appointment details (day, time), one practical instruction, a polite closing.
  • Model: "Dear Mr Larbi, … I am writing to confirm your appointment with Dr Meziane on Tuesday at 10:00. Please arrive fifteen minutes early and bring your health insurance card. If you have any questions…, do not hesitate to contact reception. Take care… Kind regards, The Reception Team".

Self-check — answer, then tap to reveal

Rank by formality: Hi there! · How do you do? · Good morning.
Hi there! (informal) < Good morning (more formal) < How do you do? (very formal, first meeting).
What should you do before asking a patient for paperwork?
Greet them first.
Why "Mr/Mrs" rather than the first name?
It's more respectful — use the first name only if the patient invites you to.
What changes if the patient looks anxious?
Slow down your speech and keep a warm tone.
What 3 elements must every short exchange contain?
Greeting, polite request, thanks/polite closing.
What 4 things must the confirmation email contain?
A greeting, the day and time, one practical instruction, a polite closing phrase.
