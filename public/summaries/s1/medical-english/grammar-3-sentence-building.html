If you remember only this
  • English word order is rigid SVO because English has very little inflection (nouns have only 2 forms) → position conveys meaning. "The surgeon examined the patient" ≠ "The patient examined the surgeon".
  • Find S-V-O: find the verb → "Who/what + verb?" = subject → "subject + verb + whom/what?" = object.
  • Yes/No question: Aux + S + V (Does the patient have…?). Wh-: Wh + Aux + S + V (When did the symptoms start?). Negative: S + Aux + not + V.
  • Yes/no questions confirm information; wh- questions are for open-ended history-taking. Negative findings ("No evidence of fracture") are as important as positive ones.
  • Prefer active voice; keep subject and verb close together.

1–2. Subject–Verb–Object word order p. 2–4

  • A sentence is to grammar what a clinical case presentation is to medicine: it organises elements into a coherent whole.
  • Meaning depends primarily on word order, unlike richly inflected languages (Russian, Finnish, Hungarian) where endings show relationships. In documentation, this precision can affect patient safety.
  • SVO can be expanded with adverbials of time, place, manner, typically at the end.
StepQuestion"Captopril controls hypertension."
1. Find the verb—controls
2. SubjectWho/what controls?Captopril
3. ObjectCaptopril controls what?hypertension
Writing tip Don't separate subject and verb with long phrases: "The patient, after waiting for three hours…, was finally seen" (correct but awkward) → "After waiting for three hours, the patient was finally seen."

3. Sentence types p. 4–7

TypeFunctionStructureExample
DeclarativeStatement — most common in notes, letters, summariesS + V + (O/complement)The patient has hypertension.
Yes/No questionConfirm informationAux + S + V + (O)?Does the patient have hypertension? · Has she experienced chest pain?
Wh- questionOpen-ended history-takingWh + Aux + S + V?When was hypertension diagnosed? · Where does it hurt? · How long have you had this pain?
NegativeAbsence — rule out diagnoses, negative findingsS + Aux + not + V + (O)The patient does not have hypertension.
  • Declarative analyses: "The patient presents with chest pain" (S+V+prepositional phrase) · "The diagnosis was confirmed by blood tests" (passive) · "Treatment began immediately" (intransitive).
  • Negative auxiliaries: do/does not · is not · has not · will not · cannot ("The infection cannot be ruled out"). Also no + noun: "There is no sign of infection."
  • Negative findings help establish differential diagnoses.

4. Seven basic sentence patterns p. 7–9

#StructureExamples
1S + V (intransitive) — action complete in itselfBlood flows. · The patient recovered. · Treatment began immediately.
2S + V (intransitive) + Adv (how/when/where)The heart lies in the thoracic cavity. · The patient improved significantly. · The patient presents with chest pain.
3S + V (transitive) + N (DO)Gallbladder stores bile. · The surgeon performed the operation. · The doctor examines the patient.
4S + V (transitive) + N (DO) + N (IO, to whom)The nerve fibres send impulses to the brain. · The nurse gave the patient medication.
5S + V + N (DO) + N (object complement) — both nouns = same thingThe hospital named Dr. Funes the best surgeon.
6S + V (linking) + N (subject complement)The liver is an essential organ. · The patient became a diabetic. · The diagnosis is pneumonia.
7S + V (linking) + Adj/Adv (subject complement)The patient remains stable. · The test results are inconclusive.

Common errors — lecture table + formative assessment

Wrong → right
  • Does the patient has diabetes? → Does the patient have… (base form after does).
  • The patient not has a fever → does not have.
  • When the symptoms did start? → When did the symptoms start? (aux after wh-word, before subject).
  • Overuse of passive: "The patient was examined by the doctor" → "The doctor examined the patient".
  • Ambiguous order: "The treatment was prescribed by the doctor for the infection" → "The doctor prescribed treatment for the infection."
  • Subject and verb separated by a long phrase → move the phrase to the front.

Self-check — answer, then tap to reveal

Why is English word order more rigid than Russian's?
English has very little inflection (nouns only singular/plural), so word position must convey meaning.
S, V, O in "The patient reports severe pain."
S the patient · V reports · O severe pain.
Make a yes/no question: "The patient is taking medication."
Is the patient taking medication?
Wh- question with "How long": "The patient has been in pain for three days."
How long has the patient been in pain?
Make negative: "She has experienced side effects."
She has not experienced any side effects.
Yes/no vs wh- questions: what is each used for in a consultation?
Yes/no to confirm information; wh- for open-ended history-taking.
Which pattern: "The patient remains stable." / "Gallbladder stores bile." / "Blood flows."
7 (linking + adjective) · 3 (transitive + DO) · 1 (intransitive).
Pattern 6 vs pattern 7?
Both linking verbs; 6 takes a noun complement (is an essential organ), 7 an adjective (remains stable).
Why document negative findings?
They are as clinically significant as positive ones and help establish differential diagnoses.
