If you remember only this
  • 8 traditional parts of speech: noun, verb, adjective, adverb, pronoun, preposition, conjunction, interjection — plus determiners as a 9th class in modern grammars.
  • Formal medical writing prefers nominalisation (noun phrases: "the administration of medication") and the passive ("the medication was administered") to sound formal and objective.
  • Adjectives go before the noun (attributive: "severe chest pain") or after a linking verb (predicative: "the condition is stable").
  • Fixed prepositions: admitted to, diagnosed with, suffering from, presented with, treated with (drug) / for (condition), referred to, complained of, discharged from, undergo surgery (no preposition).
  • Coordinating conjunctions = FANBOYS (for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so); subordinating: because, although, since, while, when, if, unless, whereas.
  • a/an introduces something the first time, then the. Don't drop articles in formal letters ("Patient was admitted…" is note style only).

0. Why it matters p. 1

  • A single wrong word can change a clinical description, a medication order or a patient instruction: "acute pain" vs "chronic pain" — the adjective changes the whole clinical picture.
  • Objectives: identify the 8 parts of speech in a medical text, classify medical vocabulary by word class, use nouns, verbs, adjectives and adverbs in short clinical descriptions, analyse authentic medical sentences.

1. Nouns — people, places, things, ideas p. 1–2

Name patients, professionals, anatomical structures, diseases, symptoms, medications, procedures and abstract concepts.

TypeDefinitionMedical examples
CommonGeneral items, people, conceptspatient, diagnosis, medication, referral, ward, hospital, symptom, treatment
ProperSpecific people, places, organisations (capital letter)Dr Patterson, Royal Melbourne Hospital, Mayo Clinic, Mrs Smith
AbstractIdeas, conditions, states that cannot be perceived by the sensespain, anxiety, compliance, deterioration, recovery, prognosis
CompoundTwo or more words working as one nounblood pressure, discharge summary, follow-up appointment, chest pain, heart rate
Writing tip — nominalisation Prefer noun phrases in formal writing: not "She administered the medication" but "The administration of medication was completed".

2. Verbs — actions, occurrences, states p. 2–3

The verb and its tense signal past history, current status or future recommendations.

TypeRoleExamples
ActionPhysical or mental actionsexamine, diagnose, prescribe, administer, refer, discharge, monitor, treat, operate
StateConditions, relationships, states of beingpresent (with), suffer (from), require, indicate, demonstrate, exhibit, complain (of)
LinkingConnect the subject to more informationappear, remain, seem, become, feel, look

"She has been prescribed metformin 500 mg twice daily." · "The patient remains stable at the time of writing." · "The patient was seen by a specialist."

Writing tip — passive Use the passive to sound formal and objective (key feature of medical letters and reports): "The medication was administered" rather than "The nurse administered the medication".

3. Adjectives — describe and modify nouns p. 3

Give clinical detail: severity, duration, nature, characteristics.

CategoryExamples
Severity & intensitysevere, mild, moderate, acute, chronic, persistent, recurring, intermittent
Clinical state & progressionstable, deteriorating, improving, responsive, non-compliant, uncontrolled
Patient characteristicselderly, mobile, independent, ambulatory, conscious, alert
Anatomical & physiologicalcardiac, pulmonary, renal, hepatic, vascular, neurological
Placement

Attributive — before the noun: "The patient has severe chest pain."

Predicative — after a linking verb: "The patient's condition is stable."

4. Adverbs — modify verbs, adjectives, other adverbs p. 4

CategoryExamples
Frequencyregularly, occasionally, intermittently, frequently, rarely, constantly
Degree & intensitysignificantly, mildly, moderately, markedly, severely, slightly
Timecurrently, previously, recently, initially, subsequently, gradually
Mannercarefully, rapidly, slowly, effectively, promptly

Position: before the main verb ("The patient frequently visits the clinic"), after the verb ("responded promptly to treatment"), or at the beginning/end ("Recently, the patient has experienced fatigue").

5. Pronouns — replace nouns p. 4–5

TypeForms
Subject (performs the action)I, you, he, she, it, we, they
Object (receives the action)me, you, him, her, it, us, them
Possessive (ownership)my, your, his, her, its, our, their
Writing tip Once the patient's name and gender are established, use he or she consistently — don't switch pronouns.

7–8. Conjunctions and interjections p. 5–6

  • Conjunctions join words, phrases or clauses → compound and complex sentences.
    • Coordinating — FANBOYS: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so. "…referred to a specialist, but further tests were required."
    • Subordinating: because, although, since, while, when, if, unless, whereas. "Because the patient's condition deteriorated, he was admitted to intensive care."
  • Interjections express strong emotion (Oh! Ouch! Well…). Rarely used in formal medical writing; appear in doctor–patient dialogue or patient narratives: "Ouch! That injection hurt."

9. Determiners — a 9th word class p. 6–7

They identify or quantify a noun (they don't describe its qualities) and come before any adjectives in a noun phrase.

TypeFormsExample
Articlesa, an, the"a 67-year-old patient" (first mention) vs "the patient" (already introduced)
Demonstrativesthis, that, these, thosethis medication, those results
Possessivesmy, your, his, her, its, our, their, the patient'sher blood pressure
Quantifierssome, any, no, many, few, several, all, most, each, everyseveral episodes of chest pain, no known allergies
Numbersone, two, first, secondtwo tablets, the first dose
Writing tip a/an for the first mention of a patient, condition or finding → the for every later reference. Leaving the article out is acceptable in rapid clinical notes, not in formal letters, reports and referrals.

Common errors — from the formative assessment answer key

Wrong word form → correction
  • "The patient was diagnosis with pneumonia" → was diagnosed (verb, past participle).
  • "a 45-years-old woman" → a 45-year-old woman (no plural inside a compound adjective).
  • "The medication was administration by the nurse" → was administered.
  • "a history of chronic, he takes…" → chronic is an adjective: it needs a noun (chronic illness); separate the clauses with a semicolon.
  • "pain was significant improved" → significantly (an adverb modifies the verb/participle).
  • "despite" is a preposition; "because" is a conjunction; "poorly" is an adverb.

What does FANBOYS stand for?
for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so — the coordinating conjunctions.
Where are interjections used in medicine?
Rarely in formal writing; in doctor–patient dialogue or patient narratives.
How do determiners differ from adjectives?
They identify or quantify the noun rather than describe it, and must come before any adjectives.
Why "a 67-year-old patient" first, then "the patient"?
a/an = new, non-specific information; the = already identified.
