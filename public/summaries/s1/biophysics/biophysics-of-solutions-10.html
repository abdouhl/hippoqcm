If you remember only this
  • Biological membrane = selective ⇒ reflection coefficient σ: Jd = LσΔπ (σ only on the osmotic flux), Jc = LΔP.
  • Darcy's law: Jc = (PfVme/RT)ΔP, so L = PfVme/RT; Pf = filtration coefficient (m/s), Vme = molar volume of water (18 cm³).
  • Solute dragged by the solvent: Jcs = (1 − σ)JnetC̄s; (1 − σ) = transmission coefficient. Impermeable solute σ = 1 (albumin), freely crossing σ = 0 (glucose).
  • Starling: water exchange plasma ↔ interstitial medium; capillary wall = dialysis membrane. Δπ = ωpRT (proteins only) pulls water into the plasma; ΔP pushes it out.
  • Normal values: ΔPa ≈ 40 mmHg, ΔPv ≈ 10 mmHg, Δπ ≈ 25 mmHg. Arteriolar end: water leaves the plasma; venular end: water returns.
  • No accumulation when Jnet,a = Jnet,v ⇔ Δπ = (ΔPa + ΔPv)/2 = ΔP̄.
  • Δπ < ΔP̄ ⇒ edema (hyperhydration of the extravascular medium); Δπ > ΔP̄ ⇒ dehydration of the extravascular medium.
  • Exercise 13: albumin 20 g/L ⇒ Δπ ≈ 9.68 mmHg, ΔP̄ = 30 mmHg ⇒ edema. 1 mmHg = 1.36 cmH₂O = 133 Pa.

Med. Medical application: the Starling phenomenon slides 62–63

Starling phenomenon Describes the water exchange between plasma and the interstitial medium, the capillary wall being modeled as a dialysis membrane.
SymbolMeaning
PaBlood pressure at the entrance of the capillary (arteriolar end)

ΔPa, ΔPvΔPa = Pa − Pint · ΔPv = Pv − Pint (in general ΔP = Pcap − Pint)

Where Δπ comes from Δπ = ωRT: 2022-23 · 2023-24 · 2024-25

  • ωplasma = ωi + ωp (small ions + proteins); ωint = ω′i ≈ ωi.
  • Δπ = (ωplasma − ωint)RT = ωpRT: due to the plasma proteins only (the oncotic pressure of part 8).
  • Direction of Δπ: interstitial medium → plasma. ΔP pushes the other way, plasma → interstitial medium.

Memory trick "Too little pull ⇒ puddle": when the proteins' pull (Δπ) is below the average push (ΔP̄), water stays outside the vessels ⇒ edema.

Ex. 13 Hypoalbuminemia and edema slides 64–66 Δπ = ωRT: 2022-23 · 2023-24 · 2024-25

Data: plasma albumin 20 g/L (M = 40 000 g/mol), Pa = 45 mmHg, Pv = 15 mmHg; the wall is impermeable to albumin but lets smaller molecules through; L = 10⁻¹¹ m²·s/kg; no albumin in the interstitium, Pi = 0 (reference); 37 °C.

  1. Net water flux (dialysis membrane): Jd = LΔπ, Jc = LΔP with ΔP = Pcap − Pi = Pcap ≡ P ⇒ Jnet = L(P − Δπ) if P > Δπ, L(Δπ − P) if P < Δπ.
  2. Reflection coefficients: σalbumin = 1, σglucose = 0.
  3. Δπ: 1 mmHg = 1.36 cmH₂O = 133 Pa; Δπ = ωalRT = (Cp,al/Mal)RT = (20/40 000 × 10³ mol/m³) × 8.31 × 310 = 1288.05 Pa = 9.68 mmHg.
  4. Entrance: Pa > Δπ ⇒ Jnet,a = L(Pa − Δπ) = 10⁻¹¹ × (45 − 9.68) × 133 = 4.69 × 10⁻⁸ m/s, water plasma → interstitial medium.
  5. Exit: Pv > Δπ too ⇒ Jnet,v = 10⁻¹¹ × (15 − 9.68) × 133 = 0.707 × 10⁻⁸ m/s, water still plasma → interstitial medium.
  6. Clinical consequence: ΔP̄ = (45 + 15)/2 = 30 mmHg > Δπ ⇒ formation of edema.
By hand 20/40 000 = 0.5 mol/m³; RT ≈ 8.3 × 310 ≈ 2 580 Pa ⇒ Δπ ≈ 1 290 Pa ≈ 1 290/133 ≈ 9.7 mmHg. Then (45 − 9.7) ≈ 35 mmHg ≈ 35 × 133 ≈ 4 700 Pa ⇒ × 10⁻¹¹ ≈ 4.7 × 10⁻⁸ m/s.

Traps — no past exam question on these slides yet; these are the confusions the lesson invites

Likely traps
  • σ goes on the diffusive solvent flux (Jd = LσΔπ), not on Jc = LΔP.
  • (1 − σ) is the transmission coefficient; σ is the reflection coefficient. Impermeable ⇒ σ = 1, transmission 0.
  • Pf is in m/s; m²·s/kg is the unit of L. Vme is the molar volume of water, not of the solute. Jc is inversely proportional to T in Darcy's law.
  • Δπ = ωpRT, not (ωi + ωp)RT: the small ions are on both sides.
  • 25 mmHg is Δπ; ΔPv ≈ 10, ΔPa ≈ 40.
  • Balance condition uses the mean (ΔPa + ΔPv)/2, not the difference.
  • Edema ⇔ Δπ < ΔP̄; dehydration ⇔ Δπ > ΔP̄. Low albumin ⇒ low Δπ ⇒ edema.
  • Convert mmHg → Pa (× 133) before multiplying by L, and g/L → mol/m³ (÷ M, × 10³) before RT.
An inconsistency in the slides Slide 62 models the capillary wall as a dialysis membrane and uses Jd = LΔπ without σ, right after introducing σ for biological membranes. Follow the slide you are on: Starling (and Exercise 13) use J = L(ΔP − Δπ) with no σ.

Self-check — answer, then tap to reveal

Biological membrane: write Jd and Jc for the solvent.
Jd = LσΔπ; Jc = LΔP; Jnet = Jc + Jd.
Darcy's law, and what are Pf and Vme?
Jc = (PfVme/RT)ΔP, L = PfVme/RT; Pf = filtration coefficient in m/s; Vme = molar volume of water, 18 cm³.
Convective solute flux across a biological membrane? Name of (1 − σ)?
Jcs = (1 − σ)JnetC̄s, C̄s = (C₁s + C₂s)/2; (1 − σ) = transmission coefficient.
σ for albumin and for glucose across the capillary wall?
σalbumin = 1 (impermeable), σglucose = 0 (crosses freely).
What does the Starling phenomenon describe?
Water exchange between plasma and the interstitial medium, the capillary wall modeled as a dialysis membrane.
Why is Δπ = ωpRT? Which way does it act?
ωplasma = ωi + ωp, ωint ≈ ωi; the small ions cancel. It draws water from the interstitial medium into the plasma.
Normal ΔPa, ΔPv, Δπ?
≈ 40, 10 and 25 mmHg.
Net water flux and its direction at each end, normal conditions?
Arteriolar: L(ΔPa − Δπ), plasma → interstitium. Venular: L(Δπ − ΔPv), interstitium → plasma.
Condition for no water accumulation?
Jnet,a = Jnet,v ⇔ Δπ = (ΔPa + ΔPv)/2 = ΔP̄.
Δπ < ΔP̄? Δπ > ΔP̄?
Δπ < ΔP̄: hyperhydration of the extravascular medium (edema). Δπ > ΔP̄: dehydration of the extravascular medium.
Exercise 13: Δπ for 20 g/L albumin (M = 40 000) at 37 °C?
0.5 mol/m³ × RT ≈ 1288 Pa ≈ 9.68 mmHg.
Exercise 13: fluxes at both ends and the clinical consequence?
Entrance 4.69 × 10⁻⁸ m/s, exit 0.707 × 10⁻⁸ m/s, both plasma → interstitium; ΔP̄ = 30 mmHg > 9.68 ⇒ edema.
1 mmHg in Pa and in cmH₂O?
133 Pa; 1.36 cmH₂O.
