If you remember only this
  • Poiseuille (laminar flow only, horizontal pipe): QV = πR⁴ΔP / 8ηL. QV goes as R⁴, and inversely as η and L.
  • ΔP = P₁ − P₂ > 0: pressure decreases along the flow; ΔP is the pressure loss (overpressure).
  • Hydraulic resistance R = ΔP/QV = 8ηL/πR⁴, in Pa·s/m³. Analogy: QV ↔ current I, ΔP ↔ ΔV, ΔP = RQV ↔ ΔV = RI.
  • Solvent: ω₁ ≠ ω₂ ⇒ diffusive flux Jd from low → high concentration; P₁ ≠ P₂ ⇒ convective flux Jc from high → low pressure.
  • Porous membrane: Jc = QV/S = LΔP (m/s), with L = p·πa⁴/8ηl the convective permeability (m/(Pa·s)), p = Np/S.
  • Filtration = solvent driven by a pressure gradient, solute retained (sieving). Ultrafiltration: semipermeable membrane, pores 0.001–0.1 µm.
  • Semipermeable membrane with ΔP > Δπ: Jnet = L(ΔP − Δπ) = LPeff, in the direction of Jc ⇒ ultrafiltration; rate Quf = SLPeff.
  • Dialysis membrane: Jnet drags solute: Jcs = Jnet·C̄s, C̄s = (C₁s + C₂s)/2; net solute flux = Jcs + Jds (Jds = P|ΔCs|).

2-5. Poiseuille's formula and hydraulic resistance slide 56

Fluid flowing in a horizontal cylindrical pipe of length L and radius R, from end 1 to end 2.

QV = πR⁴8ηL ΔP valid only for laminar flows · QV: volumetric flow rate (m³/s) · η: viscosity
  • ΔP = P₁ − P₂ > 0: the pressure P decreases with the distance travelled by the fluid.
  • ΔP is called the overpressure or pressure loss.
R = ΔPQV = 8ηLπR⁴ hydraulic flow resistance · [R] = Pa·s/m³

From one pore to the membrane

The membrane has a thickness l; its Np pores are cylinders of radius a and length l; S is the membrane area.

  1. Pores per unit area: p = Np/S.
  2. Poiseuille in one pore: qV = πa⁴ΔP / 8ηl.
  3. All pores: QV = qV·Np = pS·qV ⇒ QV = S·(p·πa⁴/8ηl)·ΔP ≡ SLΔP.
  4. Divide by S: Jc = QV/S = LΔP, [Jc] = m/s.
L = p·πa⁴8ηl convective permeability of the membrane · [L] = m/(Pa·s)

· Filtration and ultrafiltration slide 58

Filtration Filtering a solution through a porous membrane = making the solvent pass by a pressure gradient while retaining the solute particles: the principle of sieving. The convective solvent flux through a porous membrane is therefore a filtration flux.
Ultrafiltration Filtration through a semipermeable membrane whose pore diameter is between 0.001 µm and 0.1 µm.

App. Semipermeable membrane: ultrafiltration slide 59

Data: ω₁ > ω₂, P₁ > P₂, and ΔP > Δπ.

  1. P₁ > P₂ ⇒ water (1) → (2): Jc = LΔP, ΔP = P₁ − P₂.
  2. ω₁ > ω₂ ⇒ water (2) → (1): Jd = LΔπ, Δπ = (ω₁ − ω₂)RT.
  3. Net solvent flux (vector sum): Jnet = Jc + Jd.
  4. ΔP > Δπ ⇒ Jc > Jd ⇒ Jnet is in the direction of Jc ⇒ ultrafiltration.
Jnet = Jc − Jd = L(ΔP − Δπ) ≡ L·Peff Peff = ΔP − Δπ: effective filtration pressure
Q = J·S ⇒ Quf = S·L·Peff ultrafiltration rate
Memory trick "Pressure pushes, osmosis pulls": Jc goes away from high pressure, Jd goes toward high osmolarity. Same coefficient L for both, so just compare ΔP with Δπ.

Traps — no past exam question on these slides yet; these are the confusions the lesson invites

Likely traps
  • "QV is proportional to η": false, η is in the denominator. Viscosity raises R.
  • "Poiseuille applies to any flow": false, laminar flows only.
  • "Halving the radius halves the flow": false, it divides it by 16 (R⁴).
  • "The pressure increases along the flow": false, ΔP = P₁ − P₂ > 0, P decreases.
  • "Current I ↔ pressure": false, I ↔ flow rate QV; ΔV ↔ ΔP.
  • "The diffusive solvent flux goes from high to low concentration": false, solvent (water) diffuses from low → high concentration; the convective flux goes from high → low pressure.
  • Units: [R] = Pa·s/m³, [L] = m/(Pa·s), [Jc] = m/s. Ultrafiltration pores: 0.001–0.1 µm.
  • Δπ uses ω₁ − ω₂ (osmolarities, in osmol/m³ for SI): the same Δπ = ΔωRT examined in 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2024-25. 2022-23 · 2023-24 · 2024-25

Self-check — answer, then tap to reveal

Write Poiseuille's formula. When is it valid?
QV = πR⁴ΔP/8ηL, for laminar flow in a horizontal cylindrical pipe.
The radius of the pipe is halved, everything else equal. QV?
Divided by 2⁴ = 16.
What is ΔP called, and what is its sign?
The overpressure or pressure loss; ΔP = P₁ − P₂ > 0, P decreases along the flow.
Hydraulic resistance: formula and unit?
R = ΔP/QV = 8ηL/πR⁴, in Pa·s/m³.
Electric analogy: what corresponds to I, to ΔV, to ΔV = RI?
I ↔ QV; ΔV ↔ ΔP; ΔV = RI ↔ ΔP = RQV.
Diffusive vs convective solvent flux: cause and direction?
Diffusive: ω₁ ≠ ω₂, low → high concentration. Convective: P₁ ≠ P₂, high → low pressure.
Derive Jc for a membrane with Np pores of radius a and length l.
qV = πa⁴ΔP/8ηl; QV = pS·qV = SLΔP with p = Np/S and L = p·πa⁴/8ηl; Jc = QV/S = LΔP (m/s).
Hydraulic resistance of a pore, its conductance, and L?
8ηl/πa⁴; πa⁴/8ηl; L = p·πa⁴/8ηl is the hydraulic conductance per unit area (convective permeability, m/(Pa·s)).
Define filtration and ultrafiltration.
Filtration: solvent pushed through a porous membrane by a pressure gradient, solute retained (sieving). Ultrafiltration: through a semipermeable membrane with pores of 0.001–0.1 µm.
ω₁ > ω₂, P₁ > P₂, ΔP > Δπ: directions of Jc, Jd, Jnet?
Jc: (1) → (2); Jd: (2) → (1); Jnet = L(ΔP − Δπ) in the direction of Jc: ultrafiltration.
Effective filtration pressure and ultrafiltration rate?
Peff = ΔP − Δπ; Quf = S·L·Peff.
Dialysis membrane: how is the solute carried by the solvent flux?
Jcs = Jnet·C̄s with C̄s = (C₁s + C₂s)/2; net solute flux = Jcs + Jds, Jds = P|ΔCs|.
