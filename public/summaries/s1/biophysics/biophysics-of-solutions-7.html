If you remember only this
  • 1 F = 𝒩A × e = 96 500 C (charge of one mole of electrons). 1 mol Na⁺ = 1 Eq, 1 mol Ca²⁺ = 2 Eq.
  • Equivalent concentration Ce = |z|·C (Eq/L). Electroneutrality: Ce⁺ = Ce⁻, so Ce = 2Ce⁺ = 2Ce⁻.
  • Donnan set-up: a non-diffusible charged protein Prz− on side (2); the diffusible ions B⁺ and A⁻ move until equilibrium, and their concentrations end up unequal on the two sides.
  • At equilibrium: [A⁻]₁ > [A⁻]₂ and [B⁺]₂ > [B⁺]₁; the protein side is negative, the field E goes (1) → (2), so V₁ > V₂.
  • Nernst (each diffusible ion): V₂ − V₁ = −(RT/zF) ln(C₂/C₁) = −(kT/ze) ln(C₂/C₁); T in kelvin.
  • Gibbs-Donnan: (1/z) ln([I]₂/[I]₁) = (1/z′) ln([I′]₂/[I′]₁) → for A⁻ and B⁺: [A⁻]₂/[A⁻]₁ = [B⁺]₁/[B⁺]₂, i.e. [A⁻]₁[B⁺]₁ = [A⁻]₂[B⁺]₂.
  • Amount that crosses: x = zCC′ / (4C + zC′).

Worked exercise (slides 41–42) 1 L contains 2 mol CaCl₂ and 1 mol NaOH. Equivalent concentration?
  1. CaCl₂ → Ca²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ ⇒ [Ca²⁺] = 2 mol/L, [Cl⁻] = 4 mol/L.
  2. NaOH → Na⁺ + OH⁻ ⇒ [Na⁺] = 1 mol/L, [OH⁻] = 1 mol/L.
  3. Ce⁺ = 2 × 2 + 1 × 1 = 5 Eq/L; Ce⁻ = 1 × 4 + 1 × 1 = 5 Eq/L (electroneutral ✓).
  4. Ce = 2Ce⁺ = 10 Eq/L.

2-3-2. Nernst equation slides 42–45 2024-25 · 2025-26

The Donnan set-up

  • Two compartments (1) and (2) of equal volume V, separated by a non-sliding dialysis membrane.
  • (1): salt AB, [AB]₁ = C → A⁻ + B⁺, both diffusible.
  • (2): salt AB, [AB]₂ = C, and protein PrBz, [PrBz]₂ = C′ → Prz− + zB⁺. Prz− is non-diffusible.
Compartment (1)Compartment (2)
Initially[A⁻]₁ = C
[B⁺]₁ = C		[A⁻]₂ = C
[B⁺]₂ = C + zC′
[Prz−]₂ = C′
At equilibrium[B⁺]₁ = C + x
[A⁻]₁ = C + x		[B⁺]₂ = C + zC′ − x
[A⁻]₂ = C − x
[Prz−]₂ = C′
  1. [B⁺]₂ > [B⁺]₁ ⇒ n moles of B⁺ (concentration x = n/V) diffuse from (2) → (1).
  2. They are accompanied by n moles of A⁻ to ensure electroneutrality — A⁻ moves although it started at the same concentration on both sides.
  3. At equilibrium: [A⁻]₁ > [A⁻]₂, and still [B⁺]₂ > [B⁺]₁.

The transmembrane electric field

  • Everything happens as if the retained macro-ions Prz− in (2) attract B⁺ (force F₊ = qB⁺E) and repel A⁻ (force F₋ = qA⁻E).
  • E = transmembrane (transmembranaire) electric field; it always points from (+) to (−).
  • The membrane side in contact with Prz− is negatively charged, the other side positively. E: (1) → (2) ⇒ V₁ > V₂.
E = − V₂ − V₁l V₂ − V₁ = membrane potential · V₂, V₁ = electrochemical potentials · l = membrane thickness

Nernst equation

V₂ − V₁ = − RTzF ln C₂C₁ = − kTze ln C₂C₁ Nernst equation for diffusible ions · the two forms are equal because F = 𝒩A·e and R = 𝒩A·k ⇒ kT/ze = RT/zF
In calculations T in kelvin (17 °C = 290 K, 27 °C = 300 K); keep the sign of z; result in volts → ×1000 for mV. Keep track of which side is "2" (exams: 2 = axoplasm, 1 = outside).

2-3-3. Donnan equilibrium equation slides 45–46 2022-23 · 2024-25 · 2025-26

  1. Ion I (valence z): V₂ − V₁ = −(kT/ze) ln([I]₂/[I]₁). Ion I′ (valence z′): V₂ − V₁ = −(kT/z′e) ln([I′]₂/[I′]₁).
  2. Same membrane potential for both ⇒
1z ln [I]₂[I]₁ = 1z′ ln [I′]₂[I′]₁ Donnan equilibrium equation, or Gibbs-Donnan equation. Equivalently with Ce: since Ce(I) = |z|[I], [I]₂/[I]₁ = Ce2(I)/Ce1(I)

Application to A⁻ (z = −1) and B⁺ (z = +1)

−ln([A⁻]₂/[A⁻]₁) = ln([B⁺]₂/[B⁺]₁)⇒ln([A⁻]₂/[A⁻]₁) = ln([B⁺]₁/[B⁺]₂)⇒[A⁻]₂/[A⁻]₁ = [B⁺]₁/[B⁺]₂
  • Consequence: [A⁻]₁ > [A⁻]₂ ⇒ [B⁺]₂ > [B⁺]₁.
  • Rearranged: [A⁻]₁·[B⁺]₁ = [A⁻]₂·[B⁺]₂ — the form used in exam calculations.

Value of x

C − xC + x = C + xC − x + zC′ ⇒ x = zCC′4C + zC′
Memory trick "Protein side: positive ions pile up, negative ions are pushed out." The non-diffusible anion keeps B⁺ on its side and A⁻ away — and the side holding it is the negative one.

Exam traps — the wrong options past exams used

False statements to recognise (2024-25 Q14, only C is correct)
  • "The macromolecule is not necessarily charged" — false: it is the charged, non-diffusible Prz− that creates the field and the unequal distribution. 2024-25
  • "Only ions initially presenting a concentration gradient diffuse" — false: A⁻ starts at C on both sides, yet n moles of A⁻ cross with B⁺ to keep electroneutrality. 2024-25
  • "The Donnan ratio expresses the ratio of ion concentrations of opposite sign" — false per the key: the Donnan equation compares the concentrations of the same ion on both sides ([A⁻]₂/[A⁻]₁ = [B⁺]₁/[B⁺]₂). 2024-25
  • "The osmotic pressure is called oncotic pressure" — false per the key (oncotic pressure is not covered in these slides). 2024-25
  • ✅ True: "there is an inequality of ion concentrations of the same sign on either side of the membrane at equilibrium".
Calculation traps
  • Forgetting °C → K, or the minus sign of Nernst: the K⁺ answer is negative (−80.4 mV), the Na⁺ answer positive (+46.3 mV). 2024-25 · 2025-26
  • In Donnan exercises the compartment without protein has [cation] = [anion]; only the protein side is unequal. 2022-23 · 2025-26

Self-check — answer, then tap to reveal

What is the faraday, and its value?
The charge of one mole of electrons: 1 F = 𝒩A × e = 96 500 C.
How many equivalents in 1 mol Ca²⁺? In 1 mol/L Al³⁺?
2 Eq; Ce = 3 Eq/L.
1 L with 2 mol CaCl₂ and 1 mol NaOH: total equivalent concentration?
Ce⁺ = Ce⁻ = 5 Eq/L ⇒ Ce = 10 Eq/L.
Initial and equilibrium concentrations of the Donnan set-up?
Initially (1): A⁻ = B⁺ = C; (2): A⁻ = C, B⁺ = C + zC′, Pr = C′. Equilibrium (1): C + x each; (2): B⁺ = C + zC′ − x, A⁻ = C − x.
Why does A⁻ cross the membrane when it had no gradient?
It accompanies the n moles of B⁺ that leave (2), to ensure electroneutrality.
Which side is negative, which way does E point, which potential is higher?
The protein side (2) is negative; E points (1) → (2); V₁ > V₂.
Relation between E and the membrane potential?
E = −(V₂ − V₁)/l, l = membrane thickness.
Write the Nernst equation in its two forms.
V₂ − V₁ = −(RT/zF) ln(C₂/C₁) = −(kT/ze) ln(C₂/C₁).
Gibbs-Donnan equation, and its form for A⁻/B⁺?
(1/z) ln([I]₂/[I]₁) = (1/z′) ln([I′]₂/[I′]₁) ⇒ [A⁻]₂/[A⁻]₁ = [B⁺]₁/[B⁺]₂.
Expression of x?
x = zCC′/(4C + zC′).
