If you remember only this
  • Across a dialysis membrane: Δπ = Δωi·RT + ωp·RT. The oncotic pressure is only the protein term ωpRT: how easily proteins attract water.
  • Water goes toward the higher total osmolarity (ω₂ > ω₁ ⇒ H₂O (1) → (2)).
  • Without a charged protein, each diffusible ion just averages: C∞ = (C₁ + C₂)/2 on both sides ⇒ Δωi = 0, no membrane potential.
  • Undissociated (uncharged) protein: ions equal on both sides, Δπ = CRT (oncotic pressure only); the counter-pressure is applied on the protein side.
  • Dissociated protein PrNaz in (1), NaCl in (2): x of Cl⁻ and x of Na⁺ cross (2) → (1); at equilibrium [Na⁺]₁ = zC + x, [Cl⁻]₁ = x, [Na⁺]₂ = [Cl⁻]₂ = C′ − x: the Donnan effect. Δπ = (zC − 2C′ + 4x + C)RT.
  • The protein side is negative; E points (2) → (1) ⇒ V₂ > V₁; V₁ − V₂ = −(RT/zF) ln(C₁/C₂).
  • Same protein, same concentration on both sides ⇒ no Donnan effect, potential = 0.
  • Fluids: laminar at low velocity (parallel streamlines), turbulent at high velocity; [η] = Pl = Pa·s; perfect fluid η = 0, real fluid η ≠ 0; QV = dV/dt in m³/s.

2-4. Oncotic pressure slide 48 2024-25

Dialysis membrane between (1) and (2); compartment (2) holds a protein (osmolarity ωp) besides the diffusible ions (ωi).

  1. At equilibrium ω₂ > ω₁ ⇒ H₂O: (1) → (2).
  2. Osmotic pressure: Δπ = (ω₂ − ω₁)RT.
  3. ω₂ − ω₁ = [(ωi)₂ + ωp] − (ωi)₁ = [(ωi)₂ − (ωi)₁] + ωp ≡ Δωi + ωp.
Δπ = Δωi·RT + ωp·RT ΔωiRT: part due to the unequal diffusible ions · ωpRT: oncotic pressure
Oncotic pressure ωpRT: the osmotic pressure due to proteins. It expresses the ease with which proteins attract water.
Don't confuse Oncotic pressure is not "the osmotic pressure": it is only the protein part. Δπ equals ωpRT only when Δωi = 0 (experiment 2 below).

Ex. 13 Five experiments across a dialysis membrane slides 48–53 2022-23 · 2023-24 · 2024-25 · 2025-26

A dialysis membrane separates a chamber into (1) and (2) of equal volume, same temperature. Na⁺ and Cl⁻ cross it; proteins don't. When a diffusible ion has no reason to stay unequal, it ends at C∞ = (C1i + C2i)/2 on both sides.

Watch the numbering Here the protein is in (1); in part 7 it was in (2). Always locate the protein first: its side is the negative one.

Experiment 2 — undissociated protein in (1), NaCl in (2)

  1. Na⁺ and Cl⁻ diffuse (2) → (1) until [Na⁺]₁ = [Na⁺]₂ and [Cl⁻]₁ = [Cl⁻]₂ = C′/2.
  2. ω₁ > ω₂ ⇒ water diffuses (2) → (1); Δπ points (2) → (1).
  3. (ωi)₁ = (ωNa⁺)₁ + (ωCl⁻)₁ = C′ = (ωi)₂ ⇒ Δωi = 0 ⇒ Δπ = ωpRT = CRT.
  4. To stop the water flow: apply a pressure equal to CRT, opposite to Δπ, i.e. from (1) → (2) (on the protein side).

Experiment 3 — fully dissociated PrNaz in (1), NaCl in (2)

  1. PrNaz → Prz− + zNa⁺. Cl⁻ exists only in (2) ⇒ n moles of Cl⁻ (concentration x = n/V) diffuse (2) → (1).
  2. They are accompanied by n moles of Na⁺ at the same concentration x.
  3. Equilibrium: [Prz−]₁ = C, [Cl⁻]₁ = x, [Na⁺]₁ = zC + x; [Cl⁻]₂ = [Na⁺]₂ = C′ − x.
Δπ = [((ωi)₁ − (ωi)₂) + ωp]RT = [(zC + x + x) − (2C′ − 2x) + C]RT = (zC − 2C′ + 4x + C)RT

Membrane potential (exp. 3) slides 51–52 2024-25 · 2025-26

V₁ − V₂ = − RTzF ln C₁C₂ applied to any diffusible ion, with its own valence z
IonzV₁ − V₂
Cl⁻−1−(RT/(−1)F) ln(x/(C′ − x)) = −(RT/F) ln((C′ − x)/x)

Both give the same potential (that equality is the Donnan equation of part 7).

Experiment 4 — opposing voltage slide 52

Opposite voltage⇒V₁ − V₂ = 0⇒−(RT/zF) ln(C₁/C₂) = 0⇒C₁ = C₂
  • [Cl⁻]₁ = [Cl⁻]₂ = (C′ − x + x)/2 = C′/2.
  • [Na⁺]₁ = [Na⁺]₂ = (zC + x + C′ − x)/2 = (zC + C′)/2.

Experiment 5 — the same protein on both sides slide 53

  • [Prz−]₁ = [Prz−]₂ = C ⇒ no Donnan effect: the diffusible ions simply average.
  • Na⁺: (zC + C′ + zC)/2 = (C′ + 2zC)/2; Cl⁻: C′/2, on both sides.
  • C₁ = C₂ ⇒ V₁ − V₂ = 0: the membrane potential is zero.
Memory trick "No charge or no difference ⇒ no Donnan." An uncharged protein (exp. 2) or a charged protein on both sides (exp. 5) leaves every ion equal on both sides. You only get a Donnan effect from a charged protein on one side (exp. 3).

Memo slide 56

MembraneWhat happens
SemipermeableThe particles that cannot diffuse make water move and thus generate osmotic pressure: they are osmotically active.
Dialysis, in Donnan equilibriumAt first the diffusible particles diffuse across until equilibrium. At equilibrium they stop diffusing and, together with the non-diffusible protein, contribute to the osmotic pressure.

2-5. Notions of fluid biophysics slides 56, 55

TermDefinition
FluidsLiquids and gases
FlowThe overall motion of a fluid
StreamlineA curve tangent to the velocity vector at every point
Laminar flowAt low velocities ⇒ streamlines are parallel
Turbulent flowAt high velocities ⇒ disordered
ViscosityStickiness: resistance to the laminar flow of a fluid. η = viscosity coefficient; [η] = poiseuille (Pl), 1 Pl = 1 Pa·s
Perfect fluidZero viscosity: η = 0
Real fluidNon-zero viscosity: η ≠ 0
Volumetric flow rateQV = dV/dt, [QV] = m³/s; dV = volume of fluid passing through a given surface during dt
Units 1 Pl = 1 Pa·s (a product, not Pa/s). For QV, convert both volume (1 L = 10⁻³ m³) and time (1 min = 60 s): 5 L/min ≈ 8.3 × 10⁻⁵ m³/s.

Exam traps — the wrong options past exams used

2024-25 Q14 "In Donnan equilibrium" (only C is correct)
  • "The osmotic pressure is called oncotic pressure": false. Oncotic pressure is only the protein term ωpRT of Δπ = ΔωiRT + ωpRT. 2024-25
  • "The macromolecule is not necessarily charged": false. Exp. 2 shows that an undissociated protein leaves Na⁺ and Cl⁻ equal on both sides: no Donnan effect. 2024-25
  • "Only ions initially presenting a concentration gradient diffuse": false. Diffusible ions move together to keep electroneutrality (in exp. 3, n mol of Na⁺ accompany the n mol of Cl⁻). 2024-25
  • ✅ True: "inequality of ion concentrations of the same sign on either side at equilibrium". In exp. 3, [Na⁺]₁ = zC + x ≠ [Na⁺]₂ = C′ − x, and [Cl⁻]₁ = x ≠ [Cl⁻]₂ = C′ − x.
Calculation traps
  • Nernst: T in kelvin, keep the minus sign and the sign of z, use ln (not log). The protein side is the negative side. 2024-25 · 2025-26
  • In exp. 3 Δπ is not CRT: the unequal ions add Δωi = zC − 2C′ + 4x. Only exp. 2 (uncharged protein) gives Δπ = CRT.
  • x leaves (2) for both ions: (ωi)₂ = 2C′ − 2x, hence the 4x.

Self-check — answer, then tap to reveal

Split Δπ across a dialysis membrane. Which term is the oncotic pressure?
Δπ = ΔωiRT + ωpRT; the oncotic pressure is ωpRT, the osmotic pressure due to proteins.
ω₂ > ω₁: which way does water go?
(1) → (2), toward the higher osmolarity.
Exp. 1: NaCl 0.2 mol/L in (1), 0.1 mol/L in (2). Equilibrium?
Na⁺ = Cl⁻ = 0.15 mol/L on both sides; Δπ = 0, potential 0.
Exp. 2: undissociated protein C in (1), NaCl C′ in (2). Ions, Δπ, counter-pressure?
Na⁺ = Cl⁻ = C′/2 on both sides; Δπ = CRT, water (2) → (1); apply CRT from (1) → (2).
Exp. 3: equilibrium concentrations?
(1): Prz− = C, Cl⁻ = x, Na⁺ = zC + x. (2): Na⁺ = Cl⁻ = C′ − x.
Exp. 3: why does Na⁺ cross into (1) although (1) already holds zC of it?
The n mol of Cl⁻ that diffuse (2) → (1) are accompanied by n mol of Na⁺ (electroneutrality).
Exp. 3: Δπ?
(zC − 2C′ + 4x + C)RT.
Exp. 3: direction of E, which potential is higher, sign of V₁ − V₂?
E: (2) → (1); V₂ > V₁; V₁ − V₂ = −(RT/zF) ln(C₁/C₂) < 0.
V₁ − V₂ written with Cl⁻ and with Na⁺?
Cl⁻: −(RT/F) ln((C′ − x)/x). Na⁺: −(RT/F) ln((zC + x)/(C′ − x)).
Exp. 4: an opposite voltage is applied. New concentrations?
V₁ − V₂ = 0 ⇒ C₁ = C₂: Cl⁻ = C′/2, Na⁺ = (zC + C′)/2 on both sides.
Exp. 5: same PrNaz at C on both sides, NaCl C′ in (2)?
No Donnan effect: Na⁺ = (C′ + 2zC)/2, Cl⁻ = C′/2 on both sides; potential 0.
Semipermeable vs dialysis membrane: which particles make the osmotic pressure?
Semipermeable: the non-diffusible ones (osmotically active). Dialysis at Donnan equilibrium: the diffusible particles together with the non-diffusible protein.
Laminar vs turbulent flow?
Laminar: low velocity, parallel streamlines. Turbulent: high velocity, disordered.
Unit of η? Perfect vs real fluid?
Poiseuille, 1 Pl = 1 Pa·s. Perfect: η = 0; real: η ≠ 0.
Definition and unit of the volumetric flow rate?
QV = dV/dt, volume through a surface per unit time, in m³/s.
