If you remember only this
  • Psat (saturating vapor pressure) = pressure of a vapor in equilibrium with its liquid; depends only on temperature.
  • Evaporation continues while Pvapor < Psat; it stops at Pvapor = Psat (enclosure saturated).
  • Vapor = ideal gas: PV = nRT (SI: Pa, m³, K, R = 8.31). Maximum vaporizable amount: nmax = PsatV / RT.
  • Water: Duperray Psat = (t/100)⁴, bar and °C, valid 100–200 °C.
  • 1 bar = 10⁵ Pa ≈ 1 atm; 1 atm = 101 325 Pa = 760 mmHg; 1 Pa = 7.5 × 10⁻³ mmHg.

A. States of matter slides 1–2

Classic states: liquid, solid, gas. Also intermediate states such as liquid crystals (mobile phones: liquid + crystalline properties) and plasma, the "4th state" = an ionized gas (electrons, ions, atoms, neutral molecules).

2. Saturating vapor pressure

  • Closed chamber at a fixed temperature, vacuum above water: molecules evaporate until the space is saturated, at a pressure called saturation pressure Psat, which depends only on temperature.
  • Equilibrium: the flow liquid → gas equals the flow gas → liquid.
  • Volatile substance: Psat > Patm.
  • Pressure of a liquid column: P = ρgh (760 mm of mercury, ρ = 13.6 g/cm³ → ≈ 101 325 Pa).
Psat = ( t / 100 )⁴   (Duperray, water, 100 °C ≤ t ≤ 200 °C) Psat in bar, t in °C. Exercise 1: Psat = 1 bar → t = 100 × 11/4 = 100 °C.
Pvapor · V = n R T Evaporation continues while Pvapor < Psat; at Pvapor = Psat the enclosure is saturated.

Medical application: respiratory water loss slides 7–8

  • The body loses 2.4 L of water/day, of which 300–400 g by the lungs (alveolar evaporation) — depends on the humidity of inspired air.
  • Inspired air saturated → alveolar water loss = zero (Psat already reached). Dry air → loss maximal.

Exam method — the Q3/Q4 problem every year

Every EMD1 since 2022-23 has the same pair: (Q3) how much can evaporate, (Q4) what is the vapor pressure of a given amount.

  1. Convert to SI: T in K (t + 273), V in m³ (L × 10⁻³), P in Pa (1 bar = 10⁵ Pa; 760 Torr = 760 mmHg = 101 325 Pa, so 1 Torr ≈ 133.3 Pa).
  2. Maximum vaporizable amount: nmax = Psat·V / RT (mass = n × M).
  3. Compare with the amount you have, n:
    • n > nmax → saturated: Pvapor = Psat, liquid left = n − nmax.
    • n ≤ nmax → everything is vapor: Pvapor = nRT/V (< Psat).
Worked: EMD1 2022-23 Q3–Q4 (key B, D) 9 g water (n = 0.5 mol), V = 25 L, Psat(70 °C) = 0.350 bar.
nmax = 35 000 × 0.025 / (8.31 × 343) = 0.307 mol = 5.5 g vapor → liquid left = 9 − 5.5 ≈ 3.5 g.
At 100 °C: all-vapor pressure = 0.5 × 8.31 × 373 / 0.025 = 62 000 Pa = 0.62 bar < Psat (1 bar) → all vaporized, P = 0.62 bar.
Worked: EMD1 2025-26 Q3–Q4 (key B, E) Ether, Psat = 150 Torr at 25 °C, V = 80 L. nmax = (150 × 133.3) × 0.08 / (8.31 × 298) = 0.646 mol = 646 mmol.
0.25 mol < nmax → P = 0.25 × 8.31 × 298 / 0.08 = 7 740 Pa = 58 Torr.
Traps
  • Forgetting to convert L → m³ and °C → K (R = 8.31 is SI).
  • Using Psat when the amount is below nmax — then the vapor pressure is nRT/V, smaller than Psat.
  • Bigger enclosure (2023-24 Q4: +5 L) → recompute with the new V.

Self-check — answer, then tap to reveal

What does Psat depend on?
Only on temperature (for a given substance).
When does evaporation stop in a closed container?
When Pvapor = Psat: liquid and gas are in equilibrium, the enclosure is saturated.
Open, closed, isolated system: what do they exchange?
Open: energy and matter. Closed: energy only. Isolated: nothing.
Duperray's formula and its validity range?
Psat = (t/100)⁴, bar and °C, for water between 100 and 200 °C.
1 cal, 1 erg, 1 eV in joules?
4.184 J; 10⁻⁷ J; 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.
Why don't we lose water through the lungs when breathing saturated air?
Alveolar air is already at Psat; evaporation is impossible. With dry air the loss is maximal.
Two immiscible liquids: how many phases, how many states?
Two phases, one state (liquid).
A plasma is…?
The "4th state": an ionized gas of electrons, ions, atoms or neutral molecules.
