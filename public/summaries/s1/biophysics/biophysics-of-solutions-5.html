If you remember only this
  • Renal work to move n₀ osmoles from plasma to urine: ΔW = n₀RT ln(ωu/ωpl). Power per kidney = ½ ΔW/Δt.
  • Drinking water ↑ urine volume → ↓ ωu → ↓ renal work: protects the remaining nephrons in renal insufficiency.
  • Transport depends on particle nature (size, charge, shape), osmolarity and pressures. Passive: diffusion (Δconcentration), convection (Δpressure), migration (Δelectric potential); facilitated (transporters, no energy); active (transporters, ATP).
  • Diffusion until equal concentrations: C∞ = (C₁V₁ + C₂V₂)/(V₁ + V₂).
  • Fick: Q = dn/dt = −DS′ dC/dx (mol/s); D in m²/s. Flux Jd = Q/S (mol/s·m²).

Medical application: renal work slides 27–29 2023-24 · 2024-25

Renal work = energy spent by the kidneys to filter blood and secrete or reabsorb solutes (sodium, urea, glucose).

ΔW = n₀ R T ln( ωu / ωpl ) n₀ osmoles moved from plasma (pl) to urine (u) · ωu, ωpl = osmolarities of the solute in urine and plasma · power of each kidney P = ½ · ΔW / Δt (Δt = 24 h = 86 400 s)
Exercise 8 (slides 27–28): moderate renal insufficiency, 0.36 mol urea / 24 h, uremia 0.6 g/L, Murea = 60
  1. Urine 0.6 L/day: ωu = 0.36/0.6 = 0.6 osmol/L; plasma 0.6/60 = 0.01 osmol/L → ratio 60 → P = ½ × 8.31 × 310 × 0.36 × ln 60 / 86 400 = 21.97 mW per kidney.
  2. Drinks → urine 1.8 L/day: ω′u = 0.2 osmol/L → ratio 20 → P′ = 16.08 mW.
  3. P′ < P ⇒ W′ < W: drinking lowers urinary osmolarity and the nephrons' work — crucial to avoid exhausting the remaining nephrons.
Exam method (2023-24 Q10–Q11, 2024-25 Q10) Urine osmolarity from the freezing point: ωu = |ΔT|/K. The keys count all urine osmoles as urea: n = ωu × V.
2024-25: 1.0 L, −1.6 °C → 1.6/1.86 × 1 = 860 mmol.
2023-24: 0.8 L, −1.9 °C → ωu = 1.02 osmol/L, n ≈ 0.81 mol; blood 0.3 osmol/L, 37 °C → P = ½ × 0.81 × 8.31 × 310 × ln(1.02/0.3) / 86 400 ≈ 14.8 mW per kidney.
Traps
  • Power is per kidney: divide by 2 (the slide's 172 800 s = 2 × 86 400).
  • T = 37 °C = 310 K; Δt in seconds.

2-1. Molar flow rate of the solute slides 30–33

Same solute in (1) and (2) across a dialysis membrane of thickness l, C₁ᵢ > C₂ᵢ. By convention solute flows come first, then liquid flows. The solute diffuses (1) → (2) until concentrations are equal; total moles are conserved:

C∞ = C₁ᵢV₁ + C₂ᵢV₂V₁ + V₂    = (C₁ᵢ + C₂ᵢ)/2 if V₁ = V₂
Q = dndt = −D S′ dCdx    (Fick's law)    [Q] = mol/s D = diffusion coefficient (m²/s) · dC/dx = concentration gradient (mol/m⁴) · S′ = permeable pore surface · x-axis with origin at the membrane, unidirectional flow
Qi = −D S′ ΔCi/l = (D S′ / l) · |ΔCi|    (initial flow; ΔCi = C₂ᵢ − C₁ᵢ < 0, dx = l)

With mass concentration: Q = dm/dt = −DS′ dCp/dx, in kg/s (mass flow rate).

2-2. Diffusive flux of solute slide 33

Jd = QS = −D S′S dCdx    [Jd] = mol/s·m² Jd = molar flow rate per unit area · S = membrane surface
Memo (slide 33) Rate of X = X / time · Flux of X = X / surface.

Self-check — answer, then tap to reveal

Formula of renal work, and power of one kidney?
ΔW = n₀RT ln(ωu/ωpl); P = ½ ΔW/Δt.
Why does drinking water protect a patient with renal insufficiency?
More urine → lower urinary osmolarity of urea → smaller ratio ωu/ωpl → less renal work.
Three passive transports and their driving forces?
Diffusion (concentration), convection (pressure), migration (electric potential).
Facilitated vs active transport?
Both use membrane transporters; facilitated needs no energy, active uses ATP.
Final concentration after diffusion between V₁ and V₂?
C∞ = (C₁V₁ + C₂V₂)/(V₁ + V₂).
Fick's law and the unit of D?
Q = −DS′ dC/dx (mol/s); D in m²/s.
Molar flow rate vs diffusive flux?
Flow rate Q = per time (mol/s); flux Jd = Q/S per area (mol/s·m²).
Urine 1 L/day freezing at −1.6 °C: urea moles?
1.6/1.86 × 1 = 0.86 mol = 860 mmol.
